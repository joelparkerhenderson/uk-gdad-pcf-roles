# UK GDAD PCF Continuing Professional Development Checklist

Continuing Professional Development (CPD) is the intentional, ongoing process of tracking and documenting the skills, knowledge, and experience gained throughout a professional career, both formally and informally, beyond initial training. It functions as a structured lifelong learning framework to ensure that your practical qualifications and competencies remain up-to-date, legally compliant, and aligned with modern industry standards.

This page is a helpful checklist that you can use for your own continuing professional development. This page starts with general-purpose checklists, then provides UK GDAD PCF specific-purpose checklists.

This information is created by AI and is work in progress for educational purposes. If you intend to use it in any way, please consult with your organization's appropriate teammates, such as with human resources staff, or people and organizational development staff, or legal compliance staff.

## Types

The three main types of Continuing Professional Development (CPD) recognized by most professional bodies are structured learning (formal courses), reflective practice (learning from experience), and self-directed learning (independent research). Combining all three creates a balanced professional development record.

### Structured Learning (Formal CPD)

- [ ] Planned training with clear goals and set outcomes.
- [ ] Includes workshops, seminars, and certified online courses.
- [ ] Often ends with a test, assessment, or completion certificate.

### Reflective Practice (Reflective CPD)

- [ ] Learning by thinking critically about your daily work.
- [ ] Involves analyzing past projects, client interactions, or mistakes.
- [ ] Includes peer reviews, team discussions, and mentoring debriefs.

### Self-Directed Learning (Informal CPD)

- [ ] Independent study that you choose and plan yourself.
- [ ] Involves reading industry journals, books, or research papers.
- [ ] Includes listening to professional podcasts or watching educational videos

## Phases

### Phase 1: Reflect & Plan

- [ ] Audit Current Skills: Highlight your core professional strengths and identify immediate knowledge gaps.
- [ ] Review Industry Trends: Research emerging tools, software, or regulatory updates impacting your industry.
- [ ] Check Compliance Rules: Verify the mandatory CPD hours or points required by your licensing board.
- [ ] Define SMART Goals: Write down three specific career milestones you want to achieve this year.
- [ ] Set a CPD Budget: Allocate dedicated time and financial resources for courses or learning materials.

### Phase 2: Select & Execute

- [ ] Mix Learning Formats: Balance formal training (courses, webinars) with informal learning (industry podcasts, articles).
- [ ] Secure Formal Training: Enrol in accredited programs that offer certified PDUs, CEUs, or certificates.
- [ ] Shadow a Peer: Arrange dedicated time to observe a colleague or mentor handling a complex workflow.
- [ ] Join a Committee: Volunteer for internal project groups or external professional industry bodies.
- [ ] Read Industry Literature: Dedicate a specific block of time weekly to read peer-reviewed journals or whitepapers.

### Phase 3: Track & Apply

- [ ] Maintain a Central Log: Record dates, providers, activity descriptions, and hours inside a digital spreadsheet.
- [ ] Collect Evidence: File course certificates, receipts, event programs, and attendance confirmations immediately.
- [ ] Write Reflective Statements: Document what you learned and exactly how it changes your daily practice.
- [ ] Implement New Skills: Apply your fresh knowledge to a live workplace project within 30 days.
- [ ] Share with Your Team: Present your key takeaways during a team meeting to reinforce your learning.

## Teamwork

### Leadership & Management Transition Checklist (Moving from Maker to Manager)

- [ ] Schedule 1-on-1s: Set up recurring individual syncs with all direct reports within your first two weeks.
- [ ] Audit Team Workloads: Review current project boards to understand team capacity and operational bottlenecks.
- [ ] Delegate a Core Task: Identify one task you usually do and assign it to a team member to foster growth.
- [ ] Learn Feedback Models: Master a structured delivery framework like the SBI (Situation-Behavior-Impact) model.
- [ ] Study Department Budgets: Review operational expenses, licensing costs, and resource allocations for your team.

### Knowledge Sharing & Internal Teaching Checklist (Upskilling Your Team)

- [ ] Define Learning Objectives: Write down exactly what your team should be able to do by the end of your session.
- [ ] Build a Resource Repo: Create a shared folder with slide decks, cheat sheets, and useful web links.
- [ ] Record the Session: Save the live presentation video file for future onboarding requirements.
- [ ] Include Hands-on Practice: Dedicate 30% of the session time to an interactive, practical workshop exercise.
- [ ] Gather Anonymous Feedback: Send a 3-question survey post-session to discover how to improve your teaching style.

### Mentorship Session Checklist (For Mentees)

- [ ] Prep the Agenda: Send your mentor a brief list of discussion topics 48 hours prior to the meeting.
- [ ] Review Past Actions: Report back on the progress or outcomes of advice given during your last session.
- [ ] Bring a Current Dilemma: Present one specific, real-world workplace challenge you are currently facing.
- [ ] Document Feedback: Write down the mentor's constructive criticisms and specific action steps.
- [ ] Schedule the Next Meet: Confirm the date, time, and primary focus area for the upcoming sync.

## Events

### Reflective Writing Checklist (Post-Activity)

- [ ] Define the 'What': Write a concise summary of the activity, the core concepts covered, and the provider.
- [ ] Analyze the 'So What': Detail how this information directly connects to or challenges your current workflow.
- [ ] Plan the 'Now What': Outline three immediate changes you will make to your daily tasks based on this data.
- [ ] Identify Next Steps: Note down any follow-up questions or advanced topics you need to research next.

### Appraisal Review Checklist (For Quarterly Updates)

- [ ] Categorise Activities: Group your log items by type (formal, informal) to ensure a balanced learning mix.
- [ ] Benchmark Goal Progress: Measure your actual achievements directly against the SMART goals set previously.
- [ ] Quantify Business Impact: Identify at instances where your learning improved team efficiency or value.
- [ ] Request Feedback: Ask your line manager or peers to rate improvements in your performance over the year.

### Seminar & Conference Value Checklist (Maximising Event ROI)

- [ ] Map Out Sessions: Select specific presentations that target your current skills gaps before the event.
- [ ] Meet Your Peers: Aim to connect and exchange contact details with industry peers and event members.
- [ ] Take Active Notes: Summarise key takeaways, software tools mentioned, and action items during each talk.
- [ ] Draft an Event Brief: Write a one-page summary of core learnings to share with your wider internal team.

## UK GDAD PCF Role Skill Specifics

This last part of the checklist is written for your exact place in the UK GDAD PCF: the **Frontend developer** role at the **Lead frontend developer** level. You are an expert practitioner who sets direction and assures quality across teams, leads multiple or highly complex services, contributes to the community, and promotes knowledge sharing and good practice. Your development is now about organisational influence, mediation, and strategy.

### Role Level Focus: Lead Frontend Developer

- [ ] Set Direction Across Teams: Publish the frontend direction for your area and keep it short enough to be read.
- [ ] Assure Quality: Establish how frontend quality is checked across teams, including accessibility and performance gates.
- [ ] Lead Complex Services: Take responsibility for multiple or highly complex services, including their technical health.
- [ ] Provide Technical Leadership: Coach and mentor as a discipline, with structured sessions and agreed goals.
- [ ] Promote Knowledge Sharing: Establish a rhythm of talks, clinics, or guilds and keep it alive.
- [ ] Broaden Technical Range: Build genuine proficiency across a wide range of technical systems, not only frontend ones.
- [ ] Decide on Technology: Own the choice of frontend technology and approach, including when to write nothing new.
- [ ] Contribute to the Community: Represent your organisation in the cross-government frontend community.

### Skill: Accessibility

- [ ] Advocate for Users With Access Needs: Be the voice that keeps access needs on the agenda at senior level.
- [ ] Meet Predefined Standards: Ensure services under your direction demonstrably meet their committed standards.
- [ ] Champion Internally and Externally: Make the case beyond your organisation, including to suppliers.
- [ ] Offer Design Solutions: Do not just flag risks, propose the design that removes them.
- [ ] Recommend Testing Approach: Set the accessibility testing approach across teams, including manual and assistive technology testing.

### Skill: Communicating information

- [ ] Mediate and Mend Relationships: Learn mediation technique and use it when relationships are damaged.
- [ ] Communicate at All Levels: Practise moving between engineer, director, and public audiences without losing accuracy.
- [ ] Host High-Risk Discussions: Facilitate discussions about high risk and complexity under time pressure.
- [ ] Represent the Community: Speak on behalf of the frontend community to large audiences inside and outside government.

### Skill: Community collaboration

- [ ] Network Actively: Maintain a wide network across departments and outside government.
- [ ] Adapt Feedback for Lasting Effect: Tailor feedback so it changes behaviour rather than just being heard.
- [ ] Rectify Team Dynamics: Identify and address dynamic issues across teams, not just within one.
- [ ] Stimulate Right Responses: Turn health check findings into organisational change, not just team actions.

### Skill: Leadership and guidance (frontend developer)

- [ ] Reshape Structures: Learn how to change team and organisational structures toward sustainable designs.
- [ ] Lead on Strategy: Contribute to whole-organisation strategy, marrying business need with innovative analysis.
- [ ] Make High-Risk Decisions: Make and justify decisions with high risk, impact, and complexity.
- [ ] Build Consensus: Practise building agreement between organisations and highly independent stakeholders.
- [ ] Unblock at the Highest Level: Take on the disputes between teams or departments that nobody else will.
- [ ] Study Team Psychology: Learn group dynamics, conflict resolution, and mediation formally.
- [ ] Coach the Organisation: Teach conflict resolution and team dynamics beyond your own teams.

### Skill: Modern development standards

- [ ] Apply Standards to Complex Programmes: Identify the standards that hold up under genuine complexity.
- [ ] Lead Others in Applying Them: Achieve adoption through influence, tooling, and example.
- [ ] Own the Standards Lifecycle: Ensure standards are created, reviewed, and retired with clear ownership.
- [ ] Influence Beyond Your Organisation: Contribute to cross-government frontend standards work.

### Skill: Programming and build (frontend developer)

- [ ] Advise on Standards and Compliance: Be the authoritative voice on how standards should be applied.
- [ ] Set Team-Based Standards: Choose and justify the tools and techniques your teams share.
- [ ] Build High-Complexity Interfaces: Keep delivering genuinely complex interfaces so your advice stays grounded.
- [ ] Advise Stakeholders: Give technical advice to senior stakeholders without losing precision.

### Skill: Prototyping

- [ ] Make Prototyping Collective: Solicit prototypes across teams and create forums to compare them.
- [ ] Establish and Iterate Patterns: Own the shared patterns your teams reuse and evolve them with evidence.
- [ ] Choose Methods Deliberately: Match method to risk and coach others to do the same.
- [ ] Protect Discovery Work: Defend prototyping time when delivery pressure would squeeze it out.

### Skill: Strategic thinking

- [ ] Lead Strategy Design and Implementation: Own a strategy from drafting through to adoption and review.
- [ ] Direct Strategy Evaluation: Establish how strategies and policies get evaluated against business requirements.
- [ ] Learn Strategy Craft: Study strategy writing seriously, and practise the version that fits on one page.
- [ ] Connect to Government Direction: Track wider government digital strategy and align your direction with it.

### Skill: Systems integration (frontend developer)

- [ ] Define the Static Assets Build: Own how assets are built, versioned, and delivered across services.
- [ ] Co-ordinate the Integration: Take responsibility for all aspects of integration and interface testing.
- [ ] Configure Network Parts: Own the network-facing configuration and verify it under real conditions.
- [ ] Set Delivery Standards: Define caching, compression, and delivery expectations across teams.

### Skill: User focus (frontend developer)

- [ ] Represent User Needs Internally: Carry user evidence into governance and prioritisation forums.
- [ ] Distinguish Needs from Desires: Hold the line on genuine need when preference is pushed from above.
- [ ] Champion Inclusive Research: Ensure research reaches under-served users, and that findings change delivery.
- [ ] Guide Story Definition: Set expectations for how user needs are framed and prioritised across teams.
- [ ] Recommend Tools and Methods: Advise credibly on research and analysis approaches.

### Skill: Web performance optimisation

- [ ] Identify and Resolve Issues: Diagnose and fix performance problems across multiple services.
- [ ] Collect Monitoring Data: Establish real user monitoring across services and act on the trends.
- [ ] Guide on Tools and Methods: Set the performance tooling and technique direction for your teams.
- [ ] Make Performance Non-Negotiable: Establish performance budgets as a quality gate, not an aspiration.

### Skill Growth Plan: Toward Head of Frontend Development

- [ ] Review the Next Level: Read the Head of frontend development skill descriptions and mark your gaps.
- [ ] Influence Organisational Strategy: Take a role in shaping priorities beyond frontend work.
- [ ] Shape Capability: Get involved in recruitment, career paths, and internal mobility for frontend developers.
- [ ] Collaborate Across Government: Build standing relationships with counterparts in other departments.
