# Associate interaction designer - upskill resources

This page focuses on learning and growth resources for the work role of associate interaction designer.

- The resources include links to online courses, blogs, research articles, videos, and books.

- These help with job upskilling, work training, professional development, and career advancement.

- These are intended to be good starting points, not a comprehensive catalogue.

At this role level you design interactions with support and guidance, learning the GOV.UK Design System, prototyping and how to test designs with users. The resources below emphasise design fundamentals, patterns, prototyping and accessibility.

We welcome constructive feedback to improve this page and these lists.

- GitHub issues and/or pull requests are welcome.

- The resources are found via AI searches then go through human-in-the-loop proofing.

## Coursera courses

- [User Interface Design Specialization (University of Minnesota)](https://www.coursera.org/specializations/user-interface-design): Covers industry-standard theory and methods, giving fluency in the user research, prototyping and evaluation techniques needed to create intuitive interfaces.

- [User Interface Design and Prototyping (Microsoft)](https://www.coursera.org/learn/user-interface-design-and-prototyping): Covers wireframes, mockups and prototypes in Figma, including a module on design systems and style guides.

- [UX Design: Research, Prototyping, Accessible Interactions](https://www.coursera.org/learn/ux-design-research-prototyping-accessible-interactions): Combines research, prototyping and accessible interaction design rather than treating accessibility as a separate concern.

## Udemy courses

- [Figma Prototyping: A Deep Dive for UX and UI Designers](https://www.udemy.com/course/figma-prototyping-a-deep-dive-for-ux-ui-designer/): Covers basic prototypes, smart animation, reusable interactive components and interactions using variables and conditional logic.

- [Figma Advanced Course: Design, Prototyping and Responsive UI](https://www.udemy.com/course/figma-advanced-course-design-prototyping-responsive-ui/): Covers component architecture, variants and properties for reusable design systems, and using variables for colour, typography and spacing including light and dark modes.

- [Level Up Your UI/UX: Interactive Design and Prototyping in Figma](https://www.udemy.com/course/level-up-your-uiux-interactive-design-prototyping-figma/): Covers prototyping interface elements and building complex interactions such as sliders, accordions and drag and drop.

## MIT OpenCourseWare courses

- [6.831 User Interface Design and Implementation](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-831-user-interface-design-and-implementation-spring-2011/): Covers human capabilities including perception, motor skills, colour vision, attention and human error, alongside design principles, prototyping techniques and evaluation methods including heuristic evaluation and user testing.

- [6.831 Lecture Notes: Usability and Efficiency](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-831-user-interface-design-and-implementation-spring-2011/pages/lecture-notes/): The full lecture set, including the evaluation and human capability material, freely available.

## Blog posts

- [GOV.UK Design System: Patterns](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/): Best practice design solutions for specific user tasks and page types, each supported by written guidance and coded examples. The starting point for most government interaction design.

- [GOV.UK Design System: Prototyping](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/get-started/prototyping/): Guidance on prototyping with the GOV.UK Prototype Kit, which is the standard tool for interaction designers across government.

- [GOV.UK Prototype Kit documentation](https://prototype-kit.service.gov.uk/docs/): The documentation for the kit itself, used by content, interaction and service designers, developers and researchers.

- [Five tips from five weeks using the GOV.UK Prototype Kit](https://medium.com/@onebc/five-tips-from-five-weeks-using-the-gov-uk-prototyping-kit-b63f5592cc14): A practitioner account of learning the kit, including the things the documentation does not tell you.

- [Help us shape the GOV.UK Design System](https://designnotes.blog.gov.uk/2022/02/23/help-us-shape-the-gov-uk-design-system/): On how the Design System is developed collaboratively and how teams can contribute back.

- [GOV.UK Design System accessibility strategy](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/accessibility/accessibility-strategy/): How components are tested and maintained against WCAG, and what teams still have to do themselves.

- [MOJ Design System: Prototyping](https://design-patterns.service.justice.gov.uk/prototyping/): A departmental design system extending the GOV.UK one, a useful model for domain-specific patterns.

- [Unlocking Accessibility: Frontend developers discuss semantic HTML and accessible code](https://accessibility.blog.gov.uk/2025/04/25/unlocking-accessibility-frontend-developers-discuss-semantic-html-and-accessible-code/): On progressive enhancement and what accessible implementation of a design actually requires.

- [How to make a user journey map](https://designnotes.blog.gov.uk/2016/04/21/how-to-make-a-user-journey-map/): From the head of design at the Government Digital Service, on producing journey maps that are actually used.

- [User research in government](https://userresearch.blog.gov.uk/): The cross-government research blog, since interaction design decisions should be grounded in research findings.

## Research articles

- [Sample size in usability studies](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2133806.2133824): A Communications of the ACM treatment of how many participants a usability study actually needs, and why the familiar answer of five is often wrong.

- [Estimating Sample Size for Usability Testing](https://www.redalyc.org/journal/5722/572262176013/html/): Reviews the Virzi, Nielsen and Landauer, and Lewis methods for estimating the sample needed to find a target proportion of problems.

- [To Intervene or Not to Intervene: An Investigation of Three Think-Aloud Protocols in Usability Testing](https://uxpajournal.org/intervene-think-aloud-protocols-usability-testing/): Compares concurrent think-aloud, speech-communication and active intervention across problem identification, task performance, participant experience and cost.

- [Effect of Thinking Aloud on UX Metrics: A Review of the Evidence](https://measuringu.com/does-ta-increase-task-times/): Reviews the surprisingly thin evidence on how think-aloud protocols affect the metrics collected alongside them.

- [Exploring Think-Alouds in Usability Testing: An International Survey](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/220300749_Exploring_Think-Alouds_in_Usability_Testing_An_International_Survey): Documents how practitioners actually run think-aloud sessions, and how much variation exists.

- [How compliance with web accessibility standards shapes the experiences of users with and without disabilities](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1071581922001756): Empirically tests whether standards compliance improves experience, which is the question behind conformance scores.

- [Accessibility engineering in web evaluation process: a systematic literature review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10209-023-00967-2): Finds that 80 per cent of accessibility evaluation studies relied on automated tooling alone, with only 8 per cent involving users.

- [The Science of Visual Data Communication: What Works](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/15291006211051956): Synthesises the evidence on what makes a visual representation communicate accurately, applicable well beyond charts.

- [A Survey of Perception-Based Visualization Studies by Task](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.07477): Organises perception research by the task the viewer is performing, which is how design decisions should be made.

- [Understanding how agile teams reach effectiveness: A systematic literature review](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1053482224000469): Reviews the evidence on what makes multidisciplinary teams effective, which shapes how design work is best embedded.

## Books on Amazon

- [About Face: The Essentials of Interaction Design](https://www.amazon.com/About-Face-Essentials-Interaction-Design/dp/1118766571): By Alan Cooper, Robert Reimann, David Cronin and Christopher Noessel. The standard reference on interaction design, covering goal-directed design and behaviour patterns.

- [Web Accessibility Cookbook: Creating Inclusive Experiences](https://www.amazon.com/Web-Accessibility-Cookbook-Inclusive-Experiences-ebook/dp/B0D74TJF5B): By Manuel Matuzovic. Covers how interface components are actually made accessible, including toggles, accordions, modals and navigation.

- [Web Accessibility by Design: Inclusive Front-End Patterns and UI Guidelines](https://www.amazon.com/Web-Accessibility-Design-Inclusive-Guidelines-ebook/dp/B0FMJSVY2D): Covers semantic markup and ARIA roles, accessible forms and navigation, colour contrast and motion control.

- [Interviewing Users: How to Uncover Compelling Insights](https://www.amazon.com/Interviewing-Users-Uncover-Compelling-Insights/dp/1933820118): By Steve Portigal. Design decisions depend on understanding users, and this is the best guide to the interview craft.

- [Observing the User Experience: A Practitioner's Guide to User Research](https://www.amazon.com/Observing-User-Experience-Practitioners-Research/dp/0123848695): Goodman, Kuniavsky and Moed. A methods reference covering when each research approach applies.

- [Content Design](https://www.amazon.com/Content-Design-Sarah-Winters/dp/1916323316): By Sarah Winters. Interface content is part of interaction design, and this is the standard treatment of getting it right.

- [User Story Mapping: Discover the Whole Story, Build the Right Product](https://www.amazon.com/User-Story-Mapping-Discover-Product-ebook/dp/B00NF07FHS): By Jeff Patton. A way of holding the whole journey in view while designing individual screens.

- [Continuous Discovery Habits](https://www.amazon.com/Continuous-Discovery-Habits-Discover-Products-ebook/dp/B094PVB97X): By Teresa Torres. A system for keeping design decisions grounded in ongoing contact with users.

- [Inspired: How to Create Tech Products Customers Love](https://www.amazon.com/INSPIRED-Create-Tech-Products-Customers-ebook/dp/B077NRB36N): By Marty Cagan. Covers how strong product teams work and where design sits within them.

- [Smashing UX Design: Foundations for Designing Online User Experiences](https://www.skillsoft.com/book/smashing-ux-design-foundations-for-designing-online-user-experiences-807d15f0-f050-11e6-8ce3-0242c0a80c07): A foundational guide across the design process, including interaction patterns.

## Professional development tips

- Learn the GOV.UK Design System properly, including why each pattern exists and what research supports it
- Get fluent with the GOV.UK Prototype Kit, since it is the standard prototyping tool across government
- Test your designs with real users early, including people using assistive technology
- Learn the human factors behind design principles rather than memorising the principles alone
- Work in the open, sharing prototypes and rationale so others can learn from them
- Ask what task the user is trying to complete rather than what screen is needed
- Get design critique regularly, and separate feedback on the work from feedback on you
