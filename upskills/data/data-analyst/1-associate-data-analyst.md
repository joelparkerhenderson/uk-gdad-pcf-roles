# Associate data analyst - upskill resources

This page focuses on learning and growth resources for the work role of associate data analyst.

- The resources include links to online courses, blogs, research articles, videos, and books.

- These help with job upskilling, work training, professional development, and career advancement.

- These are intended to be good starting points, not a comprehensive catalogue.

At this role level you carry out analysis with support, learning to source, clean and interpret data and to present findings clearly. The resources below emphasise SQL, statistical foundations, visualisation and reproducible working.

We welcome constructive feedback to improve this page and these lists.

- GitHub issues and/or pull requests are welcome.

- The resources are found via AI searches then go through human-in-the-loop proofing.

## Coursera courses

- [Google Data Analytics Professional Certificate](https://www.coursera.org/professional-certificates/google-data-analytics): Covers data cleaning, analysis and visualisation with spreadsheets, SQL, Python and Tableau, including how to present findings in dashboards and presentations.

- [Microsoft Data Analysis with SQL, Excel and Power BI](https://www.coursera.org/specializations/microsoft-data-analysis-sql-excel-powerbi): Teaches extracting data with SQL queries, analysing datasets in Excel and building interactive Power BI dashboards to communicate insight.

- [IBM Data Analyst Professional Certificate](https://www.coursera.org/professional-certificates/ibm-data-analyst): Covers Python, Excel and SQL for analysis, and visualising and presenting findings using charts in spreadsheets and business intelligence tools.

- [15.075J Statistical Thinking and Data Analysis](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/15-075j-statistical-thinking-and-data-analysis-fall-2011/): Covers applied probability, sampling, estimation, hypothesis testing, linear regression, analysis of variance, categorical data analysis and nonparametric statistics.

## Blog posts

- [The nature of Reproducible Analytical Pipelines (RAP)](https://analysisfunction.civilservice.gov.uk/blog/the-nature-of-reproducible-analytical-pipelines-rap/): From the Government Analysis Function, describing how automated pipelines replaced manual production of more than a hundred formatted tables using R and Python.

- [Reproducible Analytical Pipelines: How we saved 3 analysts 6 weeks of copying and pasting](https://blogs.gov.scot/statistics/2023/07/24/reproducible-analytical-pipelines-how-we-saved-3-analysts-6-weeks-of-copying-and-pasting/): A Scottish Government account of a real RAP implementation and the time it recovered.

- [Reproducible Analytical Pipelines on the Data in Government blog](https://dataingovernment.blog.gov.uk/category/reproducible-analytical-pipelines): The RAP category of the cross-government blog, tracking how the practice has spread across departments.

- [Using Reproducible Analytical Pipelines to improve statistics](https://code.statisticsauthority.gov.uk/case-studies/using-reproducible-analytical-pipelines-rap-to-improve-statistics): Case studies from the Office for Statistics Regulation on RAP adoption and its effects on statistical quality.

- [Reproducible Analytical Pipelines](https://www.r-bloggers.com/2026/03/reproducible-analytical-pipelines/): Explains a RAP as a largely automated, code-based process that reduces manual and ad hoc input so that the same input reliably produces the same outputs.

- [Data Ethics on the Data in Government blog](https://dataingovernment.blog.gov.uk/category/data-ethics/): Covers how ethical questions are handled in practice in UK public sector analysis.

- [6 lessons academic research tells us about making data visualizations](https://www.poynter.org/reporting-editing/2015/6-lessons-academic-research-tells-us-about-making-data-visualizations/): Summarises what the perception research actually supports, including the surprising finding that embellished charts were recalled better without loss of accuracy.

- [Perception in data visualization](https://rstudio-pubs-static.s3.amazonaws.com/368845_9c1cc6de9d984f5688872fadd719c9b4.html): Reviews how visual encodings rank for accuracy of judgement, following Cleveland and McGill, and what that means for chart choice.

- [Reproducible data analysis](https://poldrack.github.io/psych-open-science-guide/4_reproducibleanalysis.html): From the Stanford open science guide, covering the practices that let someone else obtain identical results from your code and data.

- [Data Standards on the Data in Government blog](https://dataingovernment.blog.gov.uk/category/data-standards/): Posts on cross-government data standards, which shape how analytical outputs are published and shared.

## Research articles

- [The Science of Visual Data Communication: What Works](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/15291006211051956): Franconeri, Padilla, Shah, Zacks and Hullman synthesise the evidence on what makes a visualisation work, published in Psychological Science in the Public Interest.

- [A Survey of Perception-Based Visualization Studies by Task](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.07477): Organises the perception literature by the analytical task the viewer is performing, which is more useful than organising it by chart type.

- [Data visualization in AI-assisted decision-making: a systematic review](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/communication/articles/10.3389/fcomm.2025.1605655/full): Reviews 127 papers, of which 118 were empirical, on how visualisation affects decisions taken with algorithmic support.

- [Eye of the Beholder: Towards Measuring Visualization Complexity](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.05536): Proposes ways to measure how complex a visualisation is for a viewer, which bears directly on dashboard design decisions.

- [Visual Perception and Pre-Attentive Attributes in Oncological Data Visualisation](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12292122/): An applied study of pre-attentive attributes, showing how well-designed visualisations exploit the high sensory bandwidth of vision.

- [Leakage and the reproducibility crisis in machine-learning-based science](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10499856/): Documents leakage across 17 fields and 294 papers, a warning that applies to any predictive analysis, not only to machine learning research.

- [Pitfalls in Machine Learning Research: Reexamining the Development Cycle](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.02832): Argues that applied analytical work is hindered by ad hoc design, poor data hygiene and a lack of statistical rigour in evaluation.

- [Cross-Validation, Risk Estimation, and Model Selection](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.11696): A rigorous treatment of what cross-validation actually estimates and when it misleads.

- [Evaluating Data Quality: Comparative Insights on Standards, Methodologies, and Modern Software Tools](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-9292/14/15/3038): Compares data quality standards, methodologies and tooling, relevant to any analyst who inherits data they did not collect.

- [Locating ethics in data science: responsibility and accountability in global and distributed knowledge production systems](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5124067/): Examines where responsibility actually sits when analysis is produced across distributed teams.

## Professional development tips

- Learn SQL well, because it is the single highest-return skill for an analyst in most organisations
- Write your analysis as code from the start, so that it can be rerun, reviewed and corrected
- Practise summarising a finding in one sentence before you build any chart
- Ask what decision your analysis is meant to inform, and check the answer with the person making it
- Learn the perception research behind chart choice rather than relying on default templates
- Get familiar with your organisation's data sources, including their known quality problems
- Ask for code review early, since good habits are much easier to build than to retrofit
