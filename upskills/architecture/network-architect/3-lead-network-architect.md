# Lead network architect - upskill resources

This page focuses on learning and growth resources for the work role of lead network architect.

- The resources include links to online courses, blogs, research articles, videos, and books.

- These help with job upskilling, work training, professional development, and career advancement.

- These are intended to be good starting points, not a comprehensive catalogue.

At this role level you are accountable for designs and networks representing your business area, act as a point of escalation and subject matter expert, input into governance forums that determine IT strategy, and represent network architects internally and externally. The resources below emphasise leadership, governance, influence and technical currency at scale.

We welcome constructive feedback to improve this page and these lists.

- GitHub issues and/or pull requests are welcome.

- The resources are found via AI searches then go through human-in-the-loop proofing.

## Coursera courses

- [Strategic Leadership: Impact, Change, and Decision-Making](https://www.coursera.org/specializations/strategic-leadership-impact-change-decision-making): A Dartmouth Tuck specialisation with Sydney Finkelstein covering the mistakes smart executives make, the neuroscience behind effective decision making, and how to manage and support the people around you.

- [Strategic Leadership: Influence and Thought Leadership](https://www.coursera.org/learn/strategic-leadership-influence-thought-leadership): Covers high-level influence techniques used by senior leaders, including mapping influence networks, strong framing, building legitimacy through consistency, and leading meaning-making in uncertain or crisis moments.

- [Influencing the Organisation](https://www.coursera.org/learn/influencing-the-organisation-leading-within-and-beyond-the-organisation): Covers managing stakeholders and organisational politics while mobilising people, and aligning your mission with the organisation's purpose.

## Udemy courses

- [Network Design: Zero to Hero](https://www.udemy.com/course/network-design-zero-to-hero-by-arash-deljoo/): Covers conceptual, intermediate and detailed design of network infrastructure, including internal routing, BGP routing, modern WAN connectivity, data centre design and network security.

- [Cisco Certified CCNP Enterprise 300-420 (ENSLD) Exam](https://www.udemy.com/course/cisco-certified-ccnp-enterprise-300-420-ensld-exam-2026/): Focused on the Designing Cisco Enterprise Networks exam, covering enterprise network design principles, architecture, scalability, security, automation and infrastructure services.

- [CCNP ENCOR 350-401: Complete Cisco Enterprise Networking](https://www.udemy.com/course/ccnp-encor/): Covers architecture, virtualisation, infrastructure, security and automation, with hands-on labs and design principles for two-tier and three-tier enterprise architectures.

- [Zero Trust Networking Bootcamp](https://www.skillsoft.com/channel/zero-trust-networking-bootcamp-88ff8309-cdfc-4bd6-96d1-6b4a36aac02d): A crash course in which Michael J. ## edX courses

- [Digital Networks Essentials (IMTx)](https://www.edx.org/learn/computer-programming/imt-digital-networks-essentials): Provides the vocabulary, concepts and mechanisms common to all digital networks, and explores the TCP, UDP and IP protocols that support online communication.

## Blog posts

- [SD-WAN with ZTNA: Building Zero Trust on a SASE Framework](https://blog.orixcom.com/sd-wan-sase-and-zero-trust): Explains how SD-WAN, zero trust network access and secure access service edge fit together architecturally.

- [3 Ways for Network Practitioners to Embrace DevOps with Infrastructure-as-Code](https://blogs.cisco.com/datacenter/3-new-ways-for-network-practitioners-to-embrace-devops-with-infrastructure-as-code): Practical routes into infrastructure as code for network engineers, with concrete tooling examples.

- [CI/CD for Networking: How to Treat Network Infrastructure as Code](https://www.itential.com/resource/blog/ci-cd-for-networking-how-to-treat-network-infrastructure-as-code/): Covers version control, testing and deployment pipelines applied to network configuration.

- [What Network as Code and Infrastructure as Code Mean for Network Engineers](https://opsmill.com/blog/network-as-code-infrastructure-as-code-definition/): Distinguishes the two terms and explains what changes in day-to-day work when you adopt them.

- [AI-Powered NetDevOps: The Future of Network Automation](https://networktocode.com/blog/2025-03-27-ai-netdevops-reshapes-network-automation/): Discusses how generative AI is being applied to network configuration and what that means for design and assurance practice.

## Research articles

- [A Comprehensive Survey on Data Center Network Architectures](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9197934/): Surveys data centre network architectures and the challenges of scalability, energy efficiency, congestion and cost that follow directly from architectural design choices.

- [Rethinking the Data Center Networking: Architecture, Network Protocols, and Resource Sharing](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/6990724/): An IEEE journal paper reconsidering data centre network architecture, protocols and resource sharing from first principles.

- [Data center network architecture in cloud computing: review, taxonomy, and open research issues](https://www.academia.edu/103971359/Data_center_network_architecture_in_cloud_computing_review_taxonomy_and_open_research_issues): Reviews and classifies cloud data centre network architectures and identifies unresolved research problems.

- [Exploration and Evaluation of Congestion Control Algorithms for Data Center Networks](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s42979-023-02016-4): Evaluates congestion control algorithms against the bursty, delay-sensitive and throughput-sensitive traffic characteristic of data centre networks.

- [End-to-end congestion control in datacenter networks: a survey](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11704-025-40212-y): A survey of end-to-end congestion control approaches, covering TCP Incast, TCP Outcast, buffer pressure and queue build-up.

- [Software defined networking architecture, traffic management, security, and placement: A survey](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1389128621001481): A Computer Networks survey covering SDN architecture alongside traffic management, security and controller placement.

- [Software-Defined Networking: Categories, Analysis, and Future Directions](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/22/15/5551): An open-access analysis categorising SDN approaches and identifying where the field is heading.

- [A Comprehensive Survey of Software-Defined Networking and Traditional Network Architectures: Performance, Scalability, and Latency Analysis](https://journals.ust.edu/index.php/JST/article/view/4013): Compares SDN with traditional networking across throughput, end-to-end delay including jitter, and scalability.

- [An Overview of SDN Issues: A Case Study and Performance Evaluation of a Secure OpenFlow Protocol Implementation](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-9292/14/16/3244): An open-access case study with empirical performance evaluation of a secure OpenFlow implementation.

- [Improving dynamic congestion isolation in data-center networks](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.04639v1): Proposes improved congestion isolation to give richer congestion state information to end nodes, enabling more accurate and responsive signalling.

## Books on Amazon

- [Top-Down Network Design](https://www.amazon.com/Top-Down-Network-Design-Networking-Technology-ebook/dp/B0040EK6OM): By Priscilla Oppenheimer. A practical guide to designing reliable, secure and scalable enterprise networks, starting from applications and user requirements before selecting devices and technologies.

- [Computer Networking: A Top-Down Approach](https://www.amazon.com/Computer-Networking-Top-Down-Approach-Kurose-ebook/dp/B0FP1SXZV3): By James Kurose and Keith Ross. ## Professional development tips

- Be a genuinely useful point of escalation by being clear about what you will decide and what you will delegate
- Translate other architecture teams' standards into concrete network implications before cascading them
- Build a relationship with the IT strategy governance forum before you need to influence a decision there
- Represent your architects externally, including in cross-government and supplier forums, so their work is visible
- Set design standards in writing and review against them, rather than reviewing against personal preference
- Develop successors deliberately, since network expertise is scarce and slow to grow
- Keep enough technical currency to be credible, without taking design work away from your team
