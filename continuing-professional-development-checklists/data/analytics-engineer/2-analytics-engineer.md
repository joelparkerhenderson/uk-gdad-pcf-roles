# UK GDAD PCF Continuing Professional Development Checklist

Continuing Professional Development (CPD) is the intentional, ongoing process of tracking and documenting the skills, knowledge, and experience gained throughout a professional career, both formally and informally, beyond initial training. It functions as a structured lifelong learning framework to ensure that your practical qualifications and competencies remain up-to-date, legally compliant, and aligned with modern industry standards.

This page is a helpful checklist that you can use for your own continuing professional development. This page starts with general-purpose checklists, then provides UK GDAD PCF specific-purpose checklists.

This information is created by AI and is work in progress for educational purposes. If you intend to use it in any way, please consult with your organization's appropriate teammates, such as with human resources staff, or people and organizational development staff, or legal compliance staff.

## Types

The three main types of Continuing Professional Development (CPD) recognized by most professional bodies are structured learning (formal courses), reflective practice (learning from experience), and self-directed learning (independent research). Combining all three creates a balanced professional development record.

### Structured Learning (Formal CPD)

- [ ] Planned training with clear goals and set outcomes.
- [ ] Includes workshops, seminars, and certified online courses.
- [ ] Often ends with a test, assessment, or completion certificate.

### Reflective Practice (Reflective CPD)

- [ ] Learning by thinking critically about your daily work.
- [ ] Involves analyzing past projects, client interactions, or mistakes.
- [ ] Includes peer reviews, team discussions, and mentoring debriefs.

### Self-Directed Learning (Informal CPD)

- [ ] Independent study that you choose and plan yourself.
- [ ] Involves reading industry journals, books, or research papers.
- [ ] Includes listening to professional podcasts or watching educational videos

## Phases

### Phase 1: Reflect & Plan

- [ ] Audit Current Skills: Highlight your core professional strengths and identify immediate knowledge gaps.
- [ ] Review Industry Trends: Research emerging tools, software, or regulatory updates impacting your industry.
- [ ] Check Compliance Rules: Verify the mandatory CPD hours or points required by your licensing board.
- [ ] Define SMART Goals: Write down three specific career milestones you want to achieve this year.
- [ ] Set a CPD Budget: Allocate dedicated time and financial resources for courses or learning materials.

### Phase 2: Select & Execute

- [ ] Mix Learning Formats: Balance formal training (courses, webinars) with informal learning (industry podcasts, articles).
- [ ] Secure Formal Training: Enrol in accredited programs that offer certified PDUs, CEUs, or certificates.
- [ ] Shadow a Peer: Arrange dedicated time to observe a colleague or mentor handling a complex workflow.
- [ ] Join a Committee: Volunteer for internal project groups or external professional industry bodies.
- [ ] Read Industry Literature: Dedicate a specific block of time weekly to read peer-reviewed journals or whitepapers.

### Phase 3: Track & Apply

- [ ] Maintain a Central Log: Record dates, providers, activity descriptions, and hours inside a digital spreadsheet.
- [ ] Collect Evidence: File course certificates, receipts, event programs, and attendance confirmations immediately.
- [ ] Write Reflective Statements: Document what you learned and exactly how it changes your daily practice.
- [ ] Implement New Skills: Apply your fresh knowledge to a live workplace project within 30 days.
- [ ] Share with Your Team: Present your key takeaways during a team meeting to reinforce your learning.

## Teamwork

### Leadership & Management Transition Checklist (Moving from Maker to Manager)

- [ ] Schedule 1-on-1s: Set up recurring individual syncs with all direct reports within your first two weeks.
- [ ] Audit Team Workloads: Review current project boards to understand team capacity and operational bottlenecks.
- [ ] Delegate a Core Task: Identify one task you usually do and assign it to a team member to foster growth.
- [ ] Learn Feedback Models: Master a structured delivery framework like the SBI (Situation-Behavior-Impact) model.
- [ ] Study Department Budgets: Review operational expenses, licensing costs, and resource allocations for your team.

### Knowledge Sharing & Internal Teaching Checklist (Upskilling Your Team)

- [ ] Define Learning Objectives: Write down exactly what your team should be able to do by the end of your session.
- [ ] Build a Resource Repo: Create a shared folder with slide decks, cheat sheets, and useful web links.
- [ ] Record the Session: Save the live presentation video file for future onboarding requirements.
- [ ] Include Hands-on Practice: Dedicate 30% of the session time to an interactive, practical workshop exercise.
- [ ] Gather Anonymous Feedback: Send a 3-question survey post-session to discover how to improve your teaching style.

### Mentorship Session Checklist (For Mentees)

- [ ] Prep the Agenda: Send your mentor a brief list of discussion topics 48 hours prior to the meeting.
- [ ] Review Past Actions: Report back on the progress or outcomes of advice given during your last session.
- [ ] Bring a Current Dilemma: Present one specific, real-world workplace challenge you are currently facing.
- [ ] Document Feedback: Write down the mentor's constructive criticisms and specific action steps.
- [ ] Schedule the Next Meet: Confirm the date, time, and primary focus area for the upcoming sync.

## Events

### Reflective Writing Checklist (Post-Activity)

- [ ] Define the 'What': Write a concise summary of the activity, the core concepts covered, and the provider.
- [ ] Analyze the 'So What': Detail how this information directly connects to or challenges your current workflow.
- [ ] Plan the 'Now What': Outline three immediate changes you will make to your daily tasks based on this data.
- [ ] Identify Next Steps: Note down any follow-up questions or advanced topics you need to research next.

### Appraisal Review Checklist (For Quarterly Updates)

- [ ] Categorise Activities: Group your log items by type (formal, informal) to ensure a balanced learning mix.
- [ ] Benchmark Goal Progress: Measure your actual achievements directly against the SMART goals set previously.
- [ ] Quantify Business Impact: Identify at instances where your learning improved team efficiency or value.
- [ ] Request Feedback: Ask your line manager or peers to rate improvements in your performance over the year.

### Seminar & Conference Value Checklist (Maximising Event ROI)

- [ ] Map Out Sessions: Select specific presentations that target your current skills gaps before the event.
- [ ] Meet Your Peers: Aim to connect and exchange contact details with industry peers and event members.
- [ ] Take Active Notes: Summarise key takeaways, software tools mentioned, and action items during each talk.
- [ ] Draft an Event Brief: Write a one-page summary of core learnings to share with your wider internal team.

## UK GDAD PCF Role Skill Specifics

This last part of the checklist is written for your exact place in the UK GDAD PCF: the **Analytics engineer** role at the **Analytics engineer** level. You develop and test data models assigned by more senior colleagues, draft documentation to standard, work with others to resolve issues and risks, support trainees, and provide training and support for data set users. Your development now is about technical competence and reliability.

### Role Level Focus: Analytics Engineer

- [ ] Develop and Test Models: Own the models you are assigned, including their tests.
- [ ] Document to Standard: Write documentation that genuinely meets your organisation's standards.
- [ ] Resolve Issues With Others: Work through issues and risks collaboratively rather than escalating early.
- [ ] Support Trainees: Help newer colleagues learn, and notice which parts are hard to explain.
- [ ] Train Data Users: Deliver training and support that reduces the questions you get asked.
- [ ] Deepen SQL and Transformation: Move beyond basics into performance and maintainability.
- [ ] Learn Data Governance Basics: Understand the classification and protection rules for your data.

### Skill: Communicating between the technical and non-technical

- [ ] Communicate Both Ways: Explain data work to technical and non-technical stakeholders equally well.
- [ ] Host Difficult Discussions: Support or host discussions where dynamics are hard.
- [ ] Advocate for the Team: Represent your team's position externally.
- [ ] Manage Differing Perspectives: Hold competing views without taking sides too early.

### Skill: Data analysis and synthesis

- [ ] Profile Real Data: Build routine skill in data profiling and source system analysis.
- [ ] Present Clear Insights: Turn findings into insight colleagues can act on.
- [ ] Learn Quality Dimensions: Measure completeness, accuracy, timeliness, and consistency.
- [ ] Trace Lineage: Follow a data item from origin to consumption and document it.

### Skill: Data innovation

- [ ] Track New Tools: Keep a watchlist and note what problem each tool would solve.
- [ ] Trial Something: Evaluate one new tool properly, including its operational cost.
- [ ] Learn From Others: Study what other departments have adopted and what it cost them.
- [ ] Share What You Learn: Write up one evaluation for your team.

### Skill: Data modelling, cleansing and enrichment

- [ ] Produce Data Models: Build models and know where each type is appropriate.
- [ ] Compare Tools and Models: Understand different tools and be able to compare models honestly.
- [ ] Reverse-Engineer From Live Systems: Derive accurate models from undocumented systems.
- [ ] Apply Industry Patterns: Use recognised modelling patterns and standards deliberately.

### Skill: Metadata management

- [ ] Use the Repository Well: Use metadata for real tasks such as integration impact analysis.
- [ ] Keep It Accurate: Take responsibility for metadata being current, not just present.
- [ ] Learn the Tooling: Understand the range of metadata tools available.
- [ ] Automate Capture: Reduce manual entry wherever the pipeline can produce it instead.

### Skill: Problem management

- [ ] Investigate Problems: Look into problems in systems, processes, and services.
- [ ] Understand Problem Levels: Distinguish strategic, tactical, and operational problems.
- [ ] Contribute to Remedies: Help implement fixes and preventative measures.
- [ ] Prevent Recurrence: Turn a recurring problem into a permanent fix.

### Skill: Programming and build (data engineering)

- [ ] Deliver Simple Programs: Design, code, test, correct, and document simple scripts under direction.
- [ ] Deepen One Language: Complete an intermediate course covering error handling, testing, and packaging.
- [ ] Test Your Code: Apply testing discipline to transformation code, not just to data.
- [ ] Learn the Pipeline: Understand how your code runs in orchestration and continuous integration.

### Skill: Testing

- [ ] Execute Test Scripts: Run test scripts correctly under supervision.
- [ ] Understand the Role of Testing: Explain how testing fits into data delivery.
- [ ] Write Data Tests: Add tests for freshness, uniqueness, and referential integrity.
- [ ] Report Results Clearly: Make findings reproducible and actionable.

- [ ] Review the Next Level: Read the Senior analytics engineer skill descriptions and mark your gaps.
- [ ] Oversee Someone's Work: Start reviewing colleagues' models and giving structured feedback.
- [ ] Build Stakeholder Relationships: Take ownership of a relationship in your defined area.
- [ ] Book a Progress Review: Walk your evidence through with your line manager.
