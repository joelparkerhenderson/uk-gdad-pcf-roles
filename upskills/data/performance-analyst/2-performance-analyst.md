# Performance analyst - upskill resources

This page focuses on learning and growth resources for the work role of performance analyst.

- The resources include links to online courses, blogs, research articles, videos, and books.

- These help with job upskilling, work training, professional development, and career advancement.

- These are intended to be good starting points, not a comprehensive catalogue.

At this role level you work independently on performance analysis for a service, designing measurement, running analysis and helping the team understand how the service performs. The resources below emphasise measurement design, experimentation and evidence-based reporting.

We welcome constructive feedback to improve this page and these lists.

- GitHub issues and/or pull requests are welcome.

- The resources are found via AI searches then go through human-in-the-loop proofing.

## Coursera courses

- [Web Analytics Specialization](https://www.coursera.org/specializations/web-analytics): Covers the four tools that define the field in practice, Google Analytics 4, Google Tag Manager, Hotjar and Ahrefs, in a single applied learning path for analysts and product professionals.

- [Product Analytics Unlocked: Metrics to Meaningful Insight](https://www.coursera.org/specializations/product-analytics-unlocked-metrics-to-meaningful-insights): Teaches turning user behaviour data into decisions, applying clustering, A/B testing and survival analysis for segmentation and retention.

- [UX Research at Scale: Surveys, Analytics, Online Testing](https://www.coursera.org/learn/ux-research-at-scale): Covers gaining insight from large numbers of users through web analytics, A/B testing and remote unmoderated studies, bridging analytics and user research.

## Udemy courses

- [Google Analytics 4 (GA4): Become a Web Analytics Specialist](https://www.udemy.com/course/google-analytics-ga4-gtm-for-beginners/): Covers GA4 and Google Tag Manager from the basics, including integration with related Google products and analysing reports to reach actionable conclusions.

- [A/B Testing and Experimentation for Beginners](https://www.udemy.com/course/ab-testing-and-experimentation-for-websites-and-marketing/): Covers aligning teams with objectives, defining goals and metrics, developing test ideas, running experiments and analysing results.

- [Product Management: Concise Guide to Product Analytics](https://www.udemy.com/course/product-analytics/): Covers active usage, core and casual use, retention and growth metrics, with a deep dive into identifying good A/B test candidates and building a test plan.

- [15.075J Statistical Thinking and Data Analysis](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/15-075j-statistical-thinking-and-data-analysis-fall-2011/): Covers applied probability, sampling, estimation, hypothesis testing, regression and analysis of variance, which underpin any honest claim about whether performance changed.

- [14.310x Data Analysis for Social Scientists](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/14-310x-data-analysis-for-social-scientists-spring-2023/): Covers regression and econometrics, design of experiments, randomised controlled trials and visualisation, taught in R with policy applications.

## Research articles

- [Seven pitfalls to avoid when running controlled experiments on the web](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/1557019.1557139): Kohavi and colleagues on the specific errors that make web experiments produce confident but wrong answers, drawn from running experiments at scale.

- [Statistical Challenges in Online Controlled Experiments: A Review of A/B Testing Methodology](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/00031305.2023.2257237): A review by Larsen, Stallrich, Sengupta, Deng, Kohavi and Stevens covering the statistical problems that recur in online experimentation.

- [Statistical Challenges in Online Controlled Experiments (preprint)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.11366): The open-access version of the above review.

- [Online Controlled Experiments and A/B Testing](https://link.springer.com/rwe/10.1007/978-1-4899-7687-1_891): Kohavi and Longbotham's reference entry defining the method, its assumptions and its limits.

- [Beyond Normality: Reliable A/B Testing with Non-Gaussian Data](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.23666): Addresses the common case where the metric distribution breaks the assumptions of the standard test.

- [A nonparametric sequential test for online randomized experiments](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1610.02490): Covers testing without fixing the sample size in advance, which matters when stakeholders want to stop early.

- [The Science of Visual Data Communication: What Works](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/15291006211051956): Synthesises the evidence on what makes a visualisation communicate accurately, directly applicable to dashboard design.

- [A Survey of Perception-Based Visualization Studies by Task](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.07477): Organises the perception literature by analytical task, which is the useful way to choose an encoding.

- [An introduction to flexible methods for policy evaluation](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.00641): Covers modern approaches to estimating policy effects when a randomised design is not available.

- [Leakage and the reproducibility crisis in machine-learning-based science](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10499856/): Documents how methodological leakage produces overoptimistic results, a warning that applies to any predictive performance claim.

## Books on Amazon

- [Trustworthy Online Controlled Experiments: A Practical Guide to A/B Testing](https://www.amazon.com/Trustworthy-Online-Controlled-Experiments-Practical-ebook/dp/B0845Y3DJV): By Ron Kohavi, Diane Tang and Ya Xu. Written by experimentation leaders at Google, LinkedIn and Microsoft, drawing on running more than twenty thousand experiments a year. The standard reference.

- [Lean Analytics: Use Data to Build a Better Startup Faster](https://www.amazon.com/Lean-Analytics-Better-Startup-Faster-ebook/dp/B00AG66LTM): By Alistair Croll and Benjamin Yoskovitz. Best known for the discipline of choosing the one metric that matters right now, which transfers directly to service teams.

- [Storytelling with Data: A Data Visualization Guide for Business Professionals](https://www.amazon.com/Storytelling-Data-Visualization-Business-Professionals-ebook/dp/B016DHQSM2): By Cole Nussbaumer Knaflic. The most widely used practical guide to turning analysis into a clear visual argument.

- [Data Visualization: A Practical Introduction](https://www.amazon.com/Data-Visualization-Introduction-Kieran-Healy/dp/0691181624): By Kieran Healy. Grounds visualisation choices in perception research and works through them in R.

- [The Visual Display of Quantitative Information](https://www.amazon.com/Visual-Display-Quantitative-Information/dp/1930824130): By Edward Tufte. The foundational text on the design of data graphics.

- [Python Data Science Handbook: Essential Tools for Working with Data](https://www.amazon.com/Python-Data-Science-Handbook-Essential/dp/1098121228): By Jake VanderPlas. Covers the Python analytical toolchain for analysts moving beyond spreadsheets and dashboards.

- [Driving Digital: The Leader's Guide to Business Transformation Through Technology](https://www.amazon.com/Driving-Digital-Business-Transformation-Technology-ebook/dp/B01N10Y94E): Isaac Sacolick on enabling data-driven organisations, useful for understanding what senior stakeholders want measurement to do.

## Professional development tips

- Design measurement into a service from the start rather than instrumenting it afterwards
- Automate recurring reporting so that your time goes into interpretation rather than production
- Learn experiment design properly, since badly run tests are worse than no tests
- Combine quantitative and qualitative evidence, because either alone is easy to misread
- Be explicit about what your data cannot tell you, especially about causation
- Build relationships with the delivery team so that findings actually change the service
- Learn the perception research behind chart choice so that your dashboards are read correctly
