# Service owner - upskill resources

This page focuses on learning and growth resources for the work role of service owner.

- The resources include links to online courses, blogs, research articles, videos, and books.

- These help with job upskilling, work training, professional development, and career advancement.

- These are intended to be good starting points, not a comprehensive catalogue.

At this role level you are accountable for a whole service end to end, its outcomes for users, its performance and its continued improvement across digital and non-digital channels. The resources below emphasise service strategy, measurement, and leading across organisational boundaries.

We welcome constructive feedback to improve this page and these lists.

- GitHub issues and/or pull requests are welcome.

- The resources are found via AI searches then go through human-in-the-loop proofing.

## Coursera courses

- [Strategic Leadership: Impact, Change, and Decision-Making](https://www.coursera.org/specializations/strategic-leadership-impact-change-decision-making): A Dartmouth Tuck specialisation with Sydney Finkelstein covering the mistakes smart executives make, the neuroscience behind effective decision making, and how to manage and support the people around you.

- [Strategic Leadership: Influence and Thought Leadership](https://www.coursera.org/learn/strategic-leadership-influence-thought-leadership): Covers high-level influence techniques used by senior leaders, including mapping influence networks, strong framing, building legitimacy through consistency, and leading meaning-making in uncertain or crisis moments.

- [Influencing the Organisation](https://www.coursera.org/learn/influencing-the-organisation-leading-within-and-beyond-the-organisation): Covers managing stakeholders and organisational politics while mobilising people, and aligning your mission with the organisation's purpose.

## Udemy courses

- [Product Management Mastery: From Discovery to Delivery](https://www.udemy.com/course/product-management-mastery-from-discovery-to-delivery/): Covers the complete product journey from early discovery through delivery, with case studies and guided activities rather than theory alone.

- [Advanced Product Management: Agile Methodologies](https://www.udemy.com/course/advanced-agile-product-management/): Covers identifying user needs and the problem space, conveying the problem to teams, and pursuing product-market fit through dual-track discovery and rapid learning cycles.

- [The Complete Agile Scrum Product Owner Masterclass](https://www.udemy.com/course/professional-scrum-product-owner/): Covers the product owner role within Scrum, including backlog management, prioritisation and working with a delivery team.

- [Software Testing Strategies Are a Leadership Decision, Not a QA Checklist](https://fullscale.io/blog/software-testing-strategies/): Argues that the people closest to the product should own its quality because they understand what failure costs.

- [The nature of Reproducible Analytical Pipelines (RAP)](https://analysisfunction.civilservice.gov.uk/blog/the-nature-of-reproducible-analytical-pipelines-rap/): Relevant to product roles whose services produce statistics or recurring analytical outputs.

## Research articles

- [Understanding how agile teams reach effectiveness: A systematic literature review](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1053482224000469): Takes stock of what the evidence actually shows about what makes agile teams effective, and where the research is still thin.

- [Do Agile Scaling Approaches Make A Difference? An Empirical Comparison of Team Effectiveness Across Popular Scaling Approaches](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06599): Surveys 15,078 members of 4,013 teams and 1,841 stakeholders, finding only minor differences in effectiveness between SAFe, LeSS, Scrum of Scrums and custom approaches.

- [Exploring software development at the very large-scale: a revelatory case study and research agenda for agile method adaptation](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10664-017-9524-2): Identifies customer involvement, software architecture and inter-team coordination as the key difficulties at large scale.

- [An Empirical Analysis of the Effect of Agile Teams on Software Productivity](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/330191532_An_Empirical_Analysis_of_the_Effect_of_Agile_Teams_on_Software_Productivity): Finds team effectiveness, team management, motivation and customer satisfaction to be the factors team members perceive as most affecting productivity.

- [The Effects of Continuous Integration on Software Development: a Systematic Literature Review](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.05451): Analyses 101 empirical studies covering development activities, process, quality assurance and defects.

- [Seven pitfalls to avoid when running controlled experiments on the web](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/1557019.1557139): Kohavi and colleagues on the specific errors that make product experiments produce confident but wrong answers.

- [Statistical Challenges in Online Controlled Experiments: A Review of A/B Testing Methodology](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/00031305.2023.2257237): A review of the recurring statistical problems in the experiments product teams rely on.

- [How enterprise architecture leads to organisational benefits](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0268401222000883): Traces how technical and organisational decisions actually translate into outcomes.

- [Achieving benefits with enterprise architecture](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0963868716302360): Finds that benefits are realised indirectly within change projects rather than through artefacts, a lesson that transfers directly to roadmaps.

- [Enterprise Architecture and IT Governance to Support the BizDevOps Approach: a Systematic Mapping Study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10796-024-10473-2): Maps how governance adapts, or fails to adapt, to continuous delivery.

## Professional development tips

- Take genuine accountability for the whole service, including the parts you do not directly control
- Be able to state the service's purpose and the outcomes it exists to achieve in plain language
- Own the service's performance data and act on it rather than only reporting it
- Build the relationships across policy, operations, technology and finance that let decisions be made
- Represent users' interests at senior level, particularly when that conflicts with organisational convenience
- Make sure the service is designed as a whole, including the non-digital parts of the journey
- Prepare for service assessments by doing the work properly rather than by preparing the presentation
