# Workplace job-specific cognitive ability assessment

A workplace job-specific cognitive ability assessment is a targeted psychometric test designed to measure the exact mental processes needed for a specific role. Unlike a generic IQ test, it uses practical workplace scenarios and data to predict job performance. [1, 2, 3, 4]

## Key Characteristics

- Role-Targeted: Questions mimic the everyday tasks and data formats of the actual job.
- Timed Format: Candidates usually have 15 to 30 minutes to complete the test online.
- Objective Scoring: Results compare candidate scores against an industry benchmark or norm group.
- Strict Validation: Questions are legally vetted to ensure they predict job success without bias. [5, 6, 7, 8, 9]

## How Different Roles Are Tested## 1. Financial Analyst (Data & Logic Focus)

- What it measures: Numerical reasoning and advanced data interpretation.
- Test scenario: The candidate receives a multi-page company financial report and a series of complex charts.
- Example question: "Based on the Q3 revenue growth chart and the 12% projected inflation rate, what is the estimated net profit for Division B in Q4?" [10, 11, 12]

## 2. Customer Support Manager (Speed & Verbal Focus)

- What it measures: Verbal reasoning, error checking, and information processing.
- Test scenario: The candidate is given a dense, text-heavy customer service policy manual and an angry client email.
- Example question: "According to Section 4.2 of the policy manual, which of the following three actions is the correct protocol for a tier-two billing dispute?"

## 3. Operational Engineer (Spatial & Mechanical Focus)

- What it measures: Spatial awareness, mechanical reasoning, and fault diagnosis.
- Test scenario: The candidate views technical blueprints, 3D assembly diagrams, or automated workflow sequences.
- Example question: "If Gear A rotates clockwise at 50 RPM, in which direction and at what speed will Gear C rotate when the lever is engaged?" [13]

## Candidate Experience and Delivery

- Pre-test Practice: Candidates receive short, ungraded practice questions to understand the interface.
- Adaptive Testing: Some modern platforms adjust question difficulty based on previous answers.
- Acuity Reports: Employers receive a breakdown showing speed versus accuracy, rather than a single score. [14, 15, 16]
