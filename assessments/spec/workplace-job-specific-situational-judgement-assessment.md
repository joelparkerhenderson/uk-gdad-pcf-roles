# Workplace job-specific situational judgement assessment

A workplace job-specific situational judgement assessment evaluates a candidate’s decision-making, interpersonal skills, and professional conduct. Unlike logic or math tests, it presents realistic workplace dilemmas to see how well a candidate's behavioral choices align with company values and job requirements. [1, 2, 3, 4, 5]

## Key Characteristics

- Scenario-Based: Questions consist of short, hypothetical workplace conflicts, ethical dilemmas, or operational challenges.
- No Single Objective Right Answer: Multiple responses may seem reasonable, but one fits the organization's culture and role best.
- Response Formats: Candidates are asked to select either the Most Effective and Least Effective actions, or Rank/Rate all options from best to worst.
- Untimed or Generous Timing: These assessments focus on natural judgment rather than speed. [6, 7, 8, 9, 10]

## How Different Roles Are Tested## 1. Software Team Lead (Project & People Management Focus)

- What it measures: Conflict resolution, resource prioritization, and stakeholder management. [11, 12]
- Test scenario: A critical product launch is in two days, but a senior developer refuses to work with a teammate due to a personal dispute, threatening a major delay.
- Example options to evaluate:
- Option A: Tell them to set aside differences until the launch, promising to mediate next week.
  - Option B: Reassign the teammate to another task and take on their development work yourself.
  - Option C: Hold an immediate, brief meeting to establish a compromise solely focused on launch dependencies.

## 2. Healthcare Ward Administrator (Empathy & Compliance Focus)

- What it measures: Patient confidentiality, safety protocols, and working under pressure. [13, 14, 15, 16]
- Test scenario: A patient's relative demands sensitive medical updates over the phone, claiming an emergency, but the patient's file explicitly restricts sharing data without written consent.
- Example options to evaluate:
- Option A: Refuse to share information, citing the data policy, and end the call.
  - Option B: Take the relative's contact details, log the request, and immediately escalate to the duty nurse.
  - Option C: Share basic, non-critical updates to calm the relative down while verifying their identity.

## 3. B2B Sales Executive (Commercial Drive & Resilience Focus)

- What it measures: Negotiation skills, relationship building, and handling rejection. [17, 18, 19]
- Test scenario: A long-term, high-value client informs you they are moving to a cheaper competitor at the end of the month.
- Example options to evaluate:
- Option A: Immediately offer a maximum discount to match the competitor's price and save the contract.
  - Option B: Schedule a call to understand their changing needs and explore alternative, high-value service tiers.
  - Option C: Accept the decision gracefully, thank them for their past business, and ask for feedback via email.

## Common Pitfalls for Candidates

- Answering Ideallistically: Choosing what sounds perfect rather than what is practically effective in a real workplace.
- Ignoring Company Culture: Failing to research whether the hiring company values aggressive individual results or collaborative teamwork.
- Selecting Passive Options: Choosing options that delay action, pass the problem to someone else, or ignore the root issue. [20]
