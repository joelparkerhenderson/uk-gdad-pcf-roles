# Senior accessibility specialist - upskill resources

This page focuses on learning and growth resources for the work role of senior accessibility specialist.

- The resources include links to online courses, blogs, research articles, videos, and books.

- These help with job upskilling, work training, professional development, and career advancement.

- These are intended to be good starting points, not a comprehensive catalogue.

At this role level you take responsibility for the accessibility position of whole services, build capability in other disciplines, and represent disabled users in design decisions. The resources below emphasise capability building, prioritisation and procurement.

We welcome constructive feedback to improve this page and these lists.

- GitHub issues and/or pull requests are welcome.

- The resources are found via AI searches then go through human-in-the-loop proofing.

## Coursera courses

- [UX Design: Research, Prototyping, Accessible Interactions](https://www.coursera.org/learn/ux-design-research-prototyping-accessible-interactions): Combines research and prototyping with accessible interaction design, treating accessibility as part of design rather than a separate check.

- [Frontend Development Specialization](https://www.coursera.org/specializations/frontend): Covers HTML, CSS, JavaScript, React and interface design, including applying accessibility best practice in implementation.

- [Human-Computer Interaction I: Fundamentals and Design Principles (GTx)](https://www.coursera.org/learn/user-interface-design-principles-prototyping-practices): Covers established design principles including feedback cycles, affordances and signifiers, which underpin why accessible design works.

## Udemy courses

- [Accessible UX Writing](https://www.udemy.com/course/accessible-ux-writing/): A focused course on writing inclusive and accessible copy, including alternative text, a common gap even in mature accessibility practice.

- [UX Writing: Microcopy, User Research, Accessibility and More](https://www.udemy.com/course/ux-writing-the-art-of-user-centered-copy/): Combines interface content craft with the accessibility practice that should inform it.

- [Become a Front-End Developer: HTML, CSS and Bootstrap](https://www.udemy.com/course/become-a-front-end-developer/): Includes accessibility and performance topics such as ARIA labels, keyboard navigation and load-time improvement, useful for specialists who need to review code.

## edX courses

- [Human-Computer Interaction I: Fundamentals and Design Principles (GTx)](https://www.edx.org/course/human-computer-interaction-i-fundamentals-design-principles): Covers established design principles including feedback cycles, direct manipulation, affordances and signifiers, drawn from Georgia Tech's postgraduate HCI course.

## MIT OpenCourseWare courses

- [6.831 User Interface Design and Implementation](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-831-user-interface-design-and-implementation-spring-2011/): Covers human capabilities including perception, motor skills, colour vision, attention and human error, alongside design principles, prototyping techniques and evaluation methods including heuristic evaluation and user testing.

- [6.831 Lecture Notes: Usability and Efficiency](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-831-user-interface-design-and-implementation-spring-2011/pages/lecture-notes/): The full lecture set, including the evaluation and human capability material, freely available.

- [GOV.UK Design System Team posts on the accessibility blog](https://accessibility.blog.gov.uk/author/gov-uk-design-system-team/): Ongoing posts from the team maintaining the components most government services are built from.

- [Content accessibility: speak plainly](https://stormid.com/blog/content-accessibility-part-1-speak-plainly/): Connects plain language directly to accessibility requirements rather than treating them as separate obligations.

- [Designing content for people with limited English](https://hodigital.blog.gov.uk/2024/09/17/designing-content-for-people-with-limited-english/): Home Office Digital on a group frequently excluded by services that are technically conformant.

- [Network Segmentation Architecture and Implementation Guide](https://www.sentinelone.com/cybersecurity-101/cybersecurity/network-segmentation/): Background for specialists who need to understand why some security controls create accessibility barriers.

## Research articles

- [How compliance with web accessibility standards shapes the experiences of users with and without disabilities](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1071581922001756): Tests empirically whether standards compliance actually improves experience, which is the question behind every conformance score.

- [Accessibility engineering in web evaluation process: a systematic literature review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10209-023-00967-2): Finds that 80 per cent of accessibility evaluation studies from 2010 to 2021 relied on automated tooling alone, only 8 per cent involved users, and 12 per cent used hybrid approaches.

- [Large-scale study of web accessibility metrics](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10209-022-00956-x): Compares accessibility metrics at scale and examines what each does and does not capture.

- [Optimising the website accessibility conformance evaluation methodology](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3493612.3520452): Proposes improvements to WCAG-EM, the standard methodology for determining conformance.

- [Automated evaluation of accessibility issues of webpage content: tool and evaluation](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-92192-5): Presents and evaluates an automated accessibility tool while being honest about what automation cannot detect.

- [Colour Contrast on the Web: A WCAG 2.1 Level AA Compliance Audit of Common Crawl's Top 500 Domains](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.24067): A large-scale audit confirming colour contrast as the most common failure, consistent with the WebAIM Million findings.

- [Accessibility Analysis of Educational Websites Using WCAG 2.0](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3696318): Finds that most evaluated sites followed less than half the guidelines, a useful corrective to assumptions about baseline compliance.

- [Empirical Studies on Web Accessibility of Educational Websites: A Systematic Literature Review](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9092982/): Reviews the empirical accessibility literature and the methods used across it.

- [WCAG-EM Overview: WCAG Evaluation Methodology](https://www.w3.org/WAI/test-evaluate/conformance/wcag-em/): The W3C methodology for determining how well a digital product conforms to WCAG, and the basis for defensible audit work.

- [Exploring the Effect of Plain Terminology on Processing and Comprehension of Administrative Texts](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/ijal.12650): A reading experiment testing whether plain terminology actually improves comprehension, relevant to cognitive accessibility.

## Books on Amazon

- [Web Accessibility Cookbook: Creating Inclusive Experiences](https://www.amazon.com/Web-Accessibility-Cookbook-Inclusive-Experiences-ebook/dp/B0D74TJF5B): By Manuel Matuzovic. Written for anyone who writes frontend code, with a strong focus on HTML and on JavaScript-powered components such as toggles, accordions, modals and navigation.

- [Web Accessibility by Design: Inclusive Front-End Patterns and UI Guidelines](https://www.amazon.com/Web-Accessibility-Design-Inclusive-Guidelines-ebook/dp/B0FMJSVY2D): Covers semantic HTML and ARIA roles, accessible forms and navigation, colour contrast and motion control, with actionable techniques.

- [Interviewing Users: How to Uncover Compelling Insights](https://www.amazon.com/Interviewing-Users-Uncover-Compelling-Insights/dp/1933820118): By Steve Portigal. Accessibility work depends on hearing from disabled users properly, and this is the best guide to the interview craft.

- [Observing the User Experience: A Practitioner's Guide to User Research](https://www.amazon.com/Observing-User-Experience-Practitioners-Research/dp/0123848695): Goodman, Kuniavsky and Moed. A methods reference covering when each research approach applies, including observational work.

- [Content Design](https://www.amazon.com/Content-Design-Sarah-Winters/dp/1916323316): By Sarah Winters. Cognitive accessibility is largely a content problem, and this is the standard treatment.

- [About Face: The Essentials of Interaction Design](https://www.amazon.com/About-Face-Essentials-Interaction-Design/dp/1118766571): Cooper, Reimann, Cronin and Noessel. Covers goal-directed design and behaviour patterns, which shape whether a service is usable at all.

- [Nicely Said: Writing for the Web with Style and Purpose](https://www.amazon.com/Nicely-Said-Writing-Purpose-Voices/dp/0321988191): Fenton and Lee. A practical guide to clear web writing, which is a substantial part of accessible communication.

- [Understanding Your Users: A Practical Guide to User Research Methods](https://www.amazon.com/Understanding-Your-Users-Interactive-Technologies/dp/0128002328): Baxter, Courage and Caine. A detailed guide to planning, running and reporting research, including with participants who need adjustments.

- [Refactoring: Improving the Design of Existing Code](https://www.amazon.com/Refactoring-Improving-Existing-Addison-Wesley-Signature-ebook/dp/B07LCM8RG2): By Martin Fowler. Useful for specialists who must recommend structural changes to code they do not own.

- [Smashing UX Design: Foundations for Designing Online User Experiences](https://www.skillsoft.com/book/smashing-ux-design-foundations-for-designing-online-user-experiences-807d15f0-f050-11e6-8ce3-0242c0a80c07): A foundational guide across the design process, including the accessibility layer.

## Professional development tips

- Take responsibility for the accessibility position of whole services, not individual audits
- Build accessibility capability in designers, developers and content colleagues rather than centralising it
- Prioritise barriers by their real impact on users, not by their WCAG level alone
- Work with procurement so that suppliers are held to accessibility requirements before contracts are signed
- Represent disabled users in design and governance decisions with evidence
- Coach others on testing technique, particularly on manual and assistive technology testing
- Keep current with assistive technology and with WCAG changes, since both move
