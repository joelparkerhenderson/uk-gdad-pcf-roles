# UK GDAD PCF Continuing Professional Development Checklist

Continuing Professional Development (CPD) is the intentional, ongoing process of tracking and documenting the skills, knowledge, and experience gained throughout a professional career, both formally and informally, beyond initial training. It functions as a structured lifelong learning framework to ensure that your practical qualifications and competencies remain up-to-date, legally compliant, and aligned with modern industry standards.

This page is a helpful checklist that you can use for your own continuing professional development. This page starts with general-purpose checklists, then provides UK GDAD PCF specific-purpose checklists.

This information is created by AI and is work in progress for educational purposes. If you intend to use it in any way, please consult with your organization's appropriate teammates, such as with human resources staff, or people and organizational development staff, or legal compliance staff.

## Types

The three main types of Continuing Professional Development (CPD) recognized by most professional bodies are structured learning (formal courses), reflective practice (learning from experience), and self-directed learning (independent research). Combining all three creates a balanced professional development record.

### Structured Learning (Formal CPD)

- [ ] Planned training with clear goals and set outcomes.
- [ ] Includes workshops, seminars, and certified online courses.
- [ ] Often ends with a test, assessment, or completion certificate.

### Reflective Practice (Reflective CPD)

- [ ] Learning by thinking critically about your daily work.
- [ ] Involves analyzing past projects, client interactions, or mistakes.
- [ ] Includes peer reviews, team discussions, and mentoring debriefs.

### Self-Directed Learning (Informal CPD)

- [ ] Independent study that you choose and plan yourself.
- [ ] Involves reading industry journals, books, or research papers.
- [ ] Includes listening to professional podcasts or watching educational videos

## Phases

### Phase 1: Reflect & Plan

- [ ] Audit Current Skills: Highlight your core professional strengths and identify immediate knowledge gaps.
- [ ] Review Industry Trends: Research emerging tools, software, or regulatory updates impacting your industry.
- [ ] Check Compliance Rules: Verify the mandatory CPD hours or points required by your licensing board.
- [ ] Define SMART Goals: Write down three specific career milestones you want to achieve this year.
- [ ] Set a CPD Budget: Allocate dedicated time and financial resources for courses or learning materials.

### Phase 2: Select & Execute

- [ ] Mix Learning Formats: Balance formal training (courses, webinars) with informal learning (industry podcasts, articles).
- [ ] Secure Formal Training: Enrol in accredited programs that offer certified PDUs, CEUs, or certificates.
- [ ] Shadow a Peer: Arrange dedicated time to observe a colleague or mentor handling a complex workflow.
- [ ] Join a Committee: Volunteer for internal project groups or external professional industry bodies.
- [ ] Read Industry Literature: Dedicate a specific block of time weekly to read peer-reviewed journals or whitepapers.

### Phase 3: Track & Apply

- [ ] Maintain a Central Log: Record dates, providers, activity descriptions, and hours inside a digital spreadsheet.
- [ ] Collect Evidence: File course certificates, receipts, event programs, and attendance confirmations immediately.
- [ ] Write Reflective Statements: Document what you learned and exactly how it changes your daily practice.
- [ ] Implement New Skills: Apply your fresh knowledge to a live workplace project within 30 days.
- [ ] Share with Your Team: Present your key takeaways during a team meeting to reinforce your learning.

## Teamwork

### Leadership & Management Transition Checklist (Moving from Maker to Manager)

- [ ] Schedule 1-on-1s: Set up recurring individual syncs with all direct reports within your first two weeks.
- [ ] Audit Team Workloads: Review current project boards to understand team capacity and operational bottlenecks.
- [ ] Delegate a Core Task: Identify one task you usually do and assign it to a team member to foster growth.
- [ ] Learn Feedback Models: Master a structured delivery framework like the SBI (Situation-Behavior-Impact) model.
- [ ] Study Department Budgets: Review operational expenses, licensing costs, and resource allocations for your team.

### Knowledge Sharing & Internal Teaching Checklist (Upskilling Your Team)

- [ ] Define Learning Objectives: Write down exactly what your team should be able to do by the end of your session.
- [ ] Build a Resource Repo: Create a shared folder with slide decks, cheat sheets, and useful web links.
- [ ] Record the Session: Save the live presentation video file for future onboarding requirements.
- [ ] Include Hands-on Practice: Dedicate 30% of the session time to an interactive, practical workshop exercise.
- [ ] Gather Anonymous Feedback: Send a 3-question survey post-session to discover how to improve your teaching style.

### Mentorship Session Checklist (For Mentees)

- [ ] Prep the Agenda: Send your mentor a brief list of discussion topics 48 hours prior to the meeting.
- [ ] Review Past Actions: Report back on the progress or outcomes of advice given during your last session.
- [ ] Bring a Current Dilemma: Present one specific, real-world workplace challenge you are currently facing.
- [ ] Document Feedback: Write down the mentor's constructive criticisms and specific action steps.
- [ ] Schedule the Next Meet: Confirm the date, time, and primary focus area for the upcoming sync.

## Events

### Reflective Writing Checklist (Post-Activity)

- [ ] Define the 'What': Write a concise summary of the activity, the core concepts covered, and the provider.
- [ ] Analyze the 'So What': Detail how this information directly connects to or challenges your current workflow.
- [ ] Plan the 'Now What': Outline three immediate changes you will make to your daily tasks based on this data.
- [ ] Identify Next Steps: Note down any follow-up questions or advanced topics you need to research next.

### Appraisal Review Checklist (For Quarterly Updates)

- [ ] Categorise Activities: Group your log items by type (formal, informal) to ensure a balanced learning mix.
- [ ] Benchmark Goal Progress: Measure your actual achievements directly against the SMART goals set previously.
- [ ] Quantify Business Impact: Identify at instances where your learning improved team efficiency or value.
- [ ] Request Feedback: Ask your line manager or peers to rate improvements in your performance over the year.

### Seminar & Conference Value Checklist (Maximising Event ROI)

- [ ] Map Out Sessions: Select specific presentations that target your current skills gaps before the event.
- [ ] Meet Your Peers: Aim to connect and exchange contact details with industry peers and event members.
- [ ] Take Active Notes: Summarise key takeaways, software tools mentioned, and action items during each talk.
- [ ] Draft an Event Brief: Write a one-page summary of core learnings to share with your wider internal team.

## UK GDAD PCF Role Skill Specifics

This last part of the checklist is written for your exact place in the UK GDAD PCF: the **Software developer** role at the **Principal developer** level, technical track. You lead and plan development across large or multiple teams, you are either an expert across many technical areas or a very deep specialist in one, and you are expected to identify, test, and champion emerging technology. Your development at this level is about strategy, influence, and building capability that outlasts you.

### Role Level Focus: Principal Developer

- [ ] Define Your Expert Profile: Decide explicitly whether you are the multi-area expert or the deep specialist, and publish what you are accountable for.
- [ ] Keep Multiple Teams Effective: Build the mechanisms, such as forums, standards, and platforms, that keep teams aligned without central control.
- [ ] Work Across Disciplines: Establish standing relationships with architecture, security, data, operations, and policy colleagues.
- [ ] Develop Organisational Capability: Take an active role in internal mobility, career path design, and recruitment of technical talent.
- [ ] Champion Emerging Technology: Run a structured evaluation of an emerging technology, including risk, cost, and exit strategy.
- [ ] Publish Your Thinking: Write for the wider profession, inside government or beyond, and invite challenge.
- [ ] Build Strategic Literacy: Learn how funding, spend controls, business cases, and commercial routes actually work.
- [ ] Grow Successors: Identify and deliberately develop people who could do your job.

### Skill: Availability and capacity management

- [ ] Manage to Business KPIs: Ensure service components across your estate meet business needs and key performance indicators.
- [ ] Set the Reliability Strategy: Define how reliability is measured, funded, and traded off across multiple services.
- [ ] Design for Systemic Failure: Study and mitigate failure modes that only appear at portfolio scale, including shared dependencies.
- [ ] Link Capacity to Investment: Turn capacity analysis into credible investment and cost-optimisation proposals.

### Skill: Development process optimisation

- [ ] Analyse Current Processes: Study delivery processes across teams with real data and honest comparison.
- [ ] Implement Optimisation Opportunities: Lead improvements that hold across multiple teams, not just the willing ones.
- [ ] Develop a Team of Experts: Build and grow the specialists who deliver service improvement, and give them authority.
- [ ] Set Policy and Standards: Establish requirements for change through proportionate policy that teams can actually follow.
- [ ] Benchmark Externally: Compare your delivery performance with other organisations and act on the difference.

### Skill: Information security

- [ ] Quality Assure Solutions: Design, review, and assure services so they are engineered against articulated security threats.
- [ ] Lead Security Practice: Set the expectations and lead others in using appropriate information security practices.
- [ ] Engage With Risk Governance: Learn how risk appetite, accreditation, and residual risk acceptance work in your organisation.
- [ ] Deepen Specialist Knowledge: Pursue advanced study in an area such as cryptography, identity, or supply chain security.

### Skill: Modern development standards

- [ ] Apply Standards to Complex Programmes: Identify the standards that hold up under genuine programme complexity.
- [ ] Lead Others in Applying Them: Make adoption happen through influence, tooling, and example across teams you do not control.
- [ ] Own the Standards Lifecycle: Ensure standards are created, reviewed, and retired with clear ownership.
- [ ] Influence Beyond Your Organisation: Contribute to cross-government or industry standards work.

### Skill: Programming and build (software engineering)

- [ ] Advise on Standards and Compliance: Be the authoritative voice on how standards and methods should be applied.
- [ ] Hold Technical Responsibility: Carry technical accountability across all stages and iterations of major development work.
- [ ] Set Team-Based Standards: Define the programming tools and techniques used across teams, with clear rationale.
- [ ] Advise Senior Stakeholders: Practise giving technical advice to directors and boards without losing precision.
- [ ] Stay Technically Real: Keep enough hands-on practice that your advice survives contact with the codebase.

### Skill: Prototyping

- [ ] Use a Variety of Methods: Maintain a broad prototyping repertoire and pick by the risk being tested.
- [ ] Share Best Practice and Coach: Teach prototyping technique across teams and communities.
- [ ] Look at Strategic Service Design: Prototype at the level of whole services and journeys, not just components.
- [ ] Prototype Policy Options: Work with policy colleagues to test whether an intent is technically deliverable at all.

### Skill: Service support

- [ ] Identify, Locate, and Fix: Retain the depth to lead technically during the hardest incidents.
- [ ] Set the Support Strategy: Define how support, on-call, and operational ownership work across your estate.
- [ ] Drive Systemic Learning: Ensure findings from incidents change design standards, not just individual services.
- [ ] Invest in Platform Reliability: Champion shared platform work that removes whole categories of operational risk.

### Skill: Systems design

- [ ] Design at High Risk and Complexity: Own designs with high levels of risk, impact, and business or technical complexity.
- [ ] Control Design Practice: Govern system design practice within your enterprise or industry architecture.
- [ ] Influence Industry Models: Contribute to industry or cross-government models for new technology application.
- [ ] Develop Implementation and Procurement Strategy: Learn commercial routes well enough to shape strategies consistent with business needs.
- [ ] Ensure Adherence: Make sure technical strategies, policies, standards, and practices are actually followed, and understand why they are not when they are not.

### Skill: Systems integration

- [ ] Define the Integration Build: Own integration approach across large or multiple teams.
- [ ] Co-ordinate Build Activities: Orchestrate build across organisational, supplier, and departmental boundaries.
- [ ] Support Integration Testing: Ensure integration testing capability exists as a funded, maintained capability.
- [ ] Govern Interfaces Strategically: Set the interoperability standards that let other organisations build on your services.

### Skill: User focus

- [ ] Represent Users at Senior Level: Bring user evidence into investment and governance decisions.
- [ ] Distinguish Needs from Desires: Hold the line on genuine user need when senior stakeholders push preference.
- [ ] Champion Inclusive Research: Make inclusive research a funded expectation, not an optional extra.
- [ ] Guide Story Definition: Set the organisational expectation for how user needs are framed and evidenced.
- [ ] Recommend Tools and Methods: Advise credibly across disciplines on research and analysis approaches.

### Skill Growth Plan: Beyond Principal Developer

- [ ] Review Adjacent Roles: Read the technical architect, enterprise architect, and head of profession descriptions to see what draws you.
- [ ] Broaden Beyond Technology: Develop your knowledge of policy, commercial, and finance so your influence is not limited to technical rooms.
- [ ] Build a Public Profile: Represent your organisation externally through talks, standards work, or open source stewardship.
- [ ] Mentor at Scale: Support several senior developers across the organisation, not only your own teams.
