# UK GDAD PCF Continuing Professional Development Checklist

Continuing Professional Development (CPD) is the intentional, ongoing process of tracking and documenting the skills, knowledge, and experience gained throughout a professional career, both formally and informally, beyond initial training. It functions as a structured lifelong learning framework to ensure that your practical qualifications and competencies remain up-to-date, legally compliant, and aligned with modern industry standards.

This page is a helpful checklist that you can use for your own continuing professional development. This page starts with general-purpose checklists, then provides UK GDAD PCF specific-purpose checklists.

This information is created by AI and is work in progress for educational purposes. If you intend to use it in any way, please consult with your organization's appropriate teammates, such as with human resources staff, or people and organizational development staff, or legal compliance staff.

## Types

The three main types of Continuing Professional Development (CPD) recognized by most professional bodies are structured learning (formal courses), reflective practice (learning from experience), and self-directed learning (independent research). Combining all three creates a balanced professional development record.

### Structured Learning (Formal CPD)

- [ ] Planned training with clear goals and set outcomes.
- [ ] Includes workshops, seminars, and certified online courses.
- [ ] Often ends with a test, assessment, or completion certificate.

### Reflective Practice (Reflective CPD)

- [ ] Learning by thinking critically about your daily work.
- [ ] Involves analyzing past projects, client interactions, or mistakes.
- [ ] Includes peer reviews, team discussions, and mentoring debriefs.

### Self-Directed Learning (Informal CPD)

- [ ] Independent study that you choose and plan yourself.
- [ ] Involves reading industry journals, books, or research papers.
- [ ] Includes listening to professional podcasts or watching educational videos

## Phases

### Phase 1: Reflect & Plan

- [ ] Audit Current Skills: Highlight your core professional strengths and identify immediate knowledge gaps.
- [ ] Review Industry Trends: Research emerging tools, software, or regulatory updates impacting your industry.
- [ ] Check Compliance Rules: Verify the mandatory CPD hours or points required by your licensing board.
- [ ] Define SMART Goals: Write down three specific career milestones you want to achieve this year.
- [ ] Set a CPD Budget: Allocate dedicated time and financial resources for courses or learning materials.

### Phase 2: Select & Execute

- [ ] Mix Learning Formats: Balance formal training (courses, webinars) with informal learning (industry podcasts, articles).
- [ ] Secure Formal Training: Enrol in accredited programs that offer certified PDUs, CEUs, or certificates.
- [ ] Shadow a Peer: Arrange dedicated time to observe a colleague or mentor handling a complex workflow.
- [ ] Join a Committee: Volunteer for internal project groups or external professional industry bodies.
- [ ] Read Industry Literature: Dedicate a specific block of time weekly to read peer-reviewed journals or whitepapers.

### Phase 3: Track & Apply

- [ ] Maintain a Central Log: Record dates, providers, activity descriptions, and hours inside a digital spreadsheet.
- [ ] Collect Evidence: File course certificates, receipts, event programs, and attendance confirmations immediately.
- [ ] Write Reflective Statements: Document what you learned and exactly how it changes your daily practice.
- [ ] Implement New Skills: Apply your fresh knowledge to a live workplace project within 30 days.
- [ ] Share with Your Team: Present your key takeaways during a team meeting to reinforce your learning.

## Teamwork

### Leadership & Management Transition Checklist (Moving from Maker to Manager)

- [ ] Schedule 1-on-1s: Set up recurring individual syncs with all direct reports within your first two weeks.
- [ ] Audit Team Workloads: Review current project boards to understand team capacity and operational bottlenecks.
- [ ] Delegate a Core Task: Identify one task you usually do and assign it to a team member to foster growth.
- [ ] Learn Feedback Models: Master a structured delivery framework like the SBI (Situation-Behavior-Impact) model.
- [ ] Study Department Budgets: Review operational expenses, licensing costs, and resource allocations for your team.

### Knowledge Sharing & Internal Teaching Checklist (Upskilling Your Team)

- [ ] Define Learning Objectives: Write down exactly what your team should be able to do by the end of your session.
- [ ] Build a Resource Repo: Create a shared folder with slide decks, cheat sheets, and useful web links.
- [ ] Record the Session: Save the live presentation video file for future onboarding requirements.
- [ ] Include Hands-on Practice: Dedicate 30% of the session time to an interactive, practical workshop exercise.
- [ ] Gather Anonymous Feedback: Send a 3-question survey post-session to discover how to improve your teaching style.

### Mentorship Session Checklist (For Mentees)

- [ ] Prep the Agenda: Send your mentor a brief list of discussion topics 48 hours prior to the meeting.
- [ ] Review Past Actions: Report back on the progress or outcomes of advice given during your last session.
- [ ] Bring a Current Dilemma: Present one specific, real-world workplace challenge you are currently facing.
- [ ] Document Feedback: Write down the mentor's constructive criticisms and specific action steps.
- [ ] Schedule the Next Meet: Confirm the date, time, and primary focus area for the upcoming sync.

## Events

### Reflective Writing Checklist (Post-Activity)

- [ ] Define the 'What': Write a concise summary of the activity, the core concepts covered, and the provider.
- [ ] Analyze the 'So What': Detail how this information directly connects to or challenges your current workflow.
- [ ] Plan the 'Now What': Outline three immediate changes you will make to your daily tasks based on this data.
- [ ] Identify Next Steps: Note down any follow-up questions or advanced topics you need to research next.

### Appraisal Review Checklist (For Quarterly Updates)

- [ ] Categorise Activities: Group your log items by type (formal, informal) to ensure a balanced learning mix.
- [ ] Benchmark Goal Progress: Measure your actual achievements directly against the SMART goals set previously.
- [ ] Quantify Business Impact: Identify at instances where your learning improved team efficiency or value.
- [ ] Request Feedback: Ask your line manager or peers to rate improvements in your performance over the year.

### Seminar & Conference Value Checklist (Maximising Event ROI)

- [ ] Map Out Sessions: Select specific presentations that target your current skills gaps before the event.
- [ ] Meet Your Peers: Aim to connect and exchange contact details with industry peers and event members.
- [ ] Take Active Notes: Summarise key takeaways, software tools mentioned, and action items during each talk.
- [ ] Draft an Event Brief: Write a one-page summary of core learnings to share with your wider internal team.

## UK GDAD PCF Role Skill Specifics

This last part of the checklist is written for your exact place in the UK GDAD PCF: the **Software developer** role at the **Senior developer** level, technical track. You now deliver and integrate software that forms a complete service, plan and lead sets of related stories, hold an understanding of the whole system, and teach it to others. Your development at this level is about depth, systems thinking, and the reach of your influence.

### Role Level Focus: Senior Developer

- [ ] Lead a Related Story Set: Plan, sequence, and lead a coherent set of stories, and reflect afterwards on what you would sequence differently.
- [ ] Own the Whole-System Picture: Maintain an accurate end-to-end model of your service, and keep it current as the system changes.
- [ ] Teach the System: Run an onboarding walkthrough of the whole service and refine it from the questions you get asked.
- [ ] Work Across Disciplines: Build a working relationship with product, design, research, and operations peers so requirements arrive well formed.
- [ ] Improve Robustness Deliberately: Set a resilience or stability goal for the year, with a measure attached, and pursue it.
- [ ] Operate What You Build: Stay on the support rota, and treat operational pain as design feedback.
- [ ] Build External Perspective: Attend a cross-government technical community and bring back one practice worth trying.

### Skill: Availability and capacity management

- [ ] Manage to Business KPIs: Own service components against explicit business needs and key performance indicators.
- [ ] Design for Failure: Study resilience patterns such as circuit breakers, bulkheads, and graceful degradation, and apply one.
- [ ] Run Capacity Modelling: Learn to model demand growth and translate it into infrastructure and cost decisions.
- [ ] Lead a Game Day: Design and facilitate a failure exercise, then turn the findings into backlog items.

### Skill: Development process optimisation

- [ ] Identify Opportunities: Work with your lead to spot process optimisation opportunities using real delivery data.
- [ ] Contribute to Implementation: Take one proposed improvement and drive it to adoption across the team.
- [ ] Study Flow: Learn value stream mapping and apply it to your team's path from idea to production.
- [ ] Measure the Effect: Show before and after numbers for an improvement you made, and share them honestly.

### Skill: Information security

- [ ] Engineer Against Named Threats: Design solutions where each security control traces to a specific threat you can articulate.
- [ ] Deepen Threat Modelling: Run structured threat modelling on a whole service, not just a component.
- [ ] Learn Assurance Routes: Understand how your department assures services, and what evidence security colleagues need from you.
- [ ] Practise Secure Architecture: Study identity, authorisation, and cryptography well enough to review designs credibly.

### Skill: Modern development standards

- [ ] Support Others Consistently: Coach colleagues so standards are applied because they are understood, not because they are enforced.
- [ ] Raise the Baseline: Identify the weakest standard in your team's practice and lead the work to strengthen it.
- [ ] Automate Enforcement: Encode standards into pipelines, templates, and scaffolding so the easy path is the right path.
- [ ] Keep Genuinely Current: Follow credible sources, evaluate new practices sceptically, and pilot the ones that fit.

### Skill: Programming and build (software engineering)

- [ ] Handle Medium-to-High Complexity Confidently: Deliver programs and scripts of real complexity, documented to the right standards.
- [ ] Review Specifications Rigorously: Challenge specifications early, and record the assumptions you had to make.
- [ ] Master a Second Paradigm: Learn a language or paradigm distinct from your main one, and note what it teaches you.
- [ ] Improve Testability by Design: Refactor a hard-to-test area so future changes are cheaper and safer.
- [ ] Own Build and Release: Understand your delivery pipeline deeply enough to redesign it, not just repair it.

### Skill: Prototyping

- [ ] Make Prototyping Collective: Solicit prototypes from the whole team and run structured sessions to compare them.
- [ ] Establish and Iterate Patterns: Codify design patterns for your service, and revise them as evidence accumulates.
- [ ] Match Method to Question: Choose deliberately among paper, code, data, and infrastructure prototypes.
- [ ] Prototype the Risky Part: Practise aiming prototypes at the biggest technical unknown rather than the easiest one.

### Skill: Service support

- [ ] Identify, Locate, and Fix: Build the diagnostic skill to move from symptom to root cause without guesswork.
- [ ] Lead an Incident: Take the technical lead on a real incident, then write the post-incident review.
- [ ] Remove Fault Classes: Analyse recurring faults and design the change that eliminates the whole category.
- [ ] Improve Observability: Add the telemetry that would have made your last hard incident easy.

### Skill: Systems design

- [ ] Design at Medium Risk and Complexity: Own the design of systems with meaningful risk, impact, and complexity.
- [ ] Select Standards and Methods: Choose design standards and tools deliberately, and make sure they are applied effectively.
- [ ] Review Others' Designs: Review colleagues' designs for appropriate technology, efficient resource use, and clean integration.
- [ ] Study Architecture Deeply: Work through a substantial architecture text or course, and apply one model to a live design.
- [ ] Write Decision Records: Capture significant decisions with context, options, and consequences so future teams understand them.

### Skill: Systems integration

- [ ] Define the Integration Build: Take responsibility for defining how components come together into a working build.
- [ ] Co-ordinate Across Systems: Practise sequencing build and release activities across teams that do not report to you.
- [ ] Support Integration Testing: Understand and support integration test approaches, including environments and test data.
- [ ] Learn Interface Governance: Study versioning, deprecation, and backwards compatibility, and apply a policy to one interface.

### Skill: User focus

- [ ] Represent Users Credibly: Speak for users in design and prioritisation discussions using evidence, not opinion.
- [ ] Distinguish Needs from Desires: Coach others to separate what users ask for from what they actually need.
- [ ] Champion Inclusive Research: Push for research that reaches users with access needs, low digital confidence, and unusual circumstances.
- [ ] Guide Story Definition: Help the team prioritise and frame stories, and explain your reasoning so others learn it.
- [ ] Recommend Tools and Methods: Know enough about research and analysis methods to advise the team well.

### Skill Growth Plan: Toward Lead Developer

- [ ] Review the Next Level: Read the Lead developer skill descriptions and mark your genuine gaps.
- [ ] Practise Technical Leadership: Guide how a team works for a defined period, with your lead observing and coaching you.
- [ ] Contribute to the Community: Speak, write, or run a session for the wider developer community.
- [ ] Choose Your Track: Reflect on whether the technical or management route fits you, and discuss it with your line manager.
