# Head of content design - upskill resources

This page focuses on learning and growth resources for the work role of head of content design.

- The resources include links to online courses, blogs, research articles, videos, and books.

- These help with job upskilling, work training, professional development, and career advancement.

- These are intended to be good starting points, not a comprehensive catalogue.

At this role level you are accountable for the content design function, its strategy, its capability and its influence on how the organisation communicates. The resources below emphasise organisational content strategy, capability building and executive influence.

We welcome constructive feedback to improve this page and these lists.

- GitHub issues and/or pull requests are welcome.

- The resources are found via AI searches then go through human-in-the-loop proofing.

## Coursera courses

- [Strategic Leadership: Impact, Change, and Decision-Making](https://www.coursera.org/specializations/strategic-leadership-impact-change-decision-making): A Dartmouth Tuck specialisation with Sydney Finkelstein covering the mistakes smart executives make, the neuroscience behind effective decision making, and how to manage and support the people around you.

- [Strategic Leadership: Influence and Thought Leadership](https://www.coursera.org/learn/strategic-leadership-influence-thought-leadership): Covers high-level influence techniques used by senior leaders, including mapping influence networks, strong framing, building legitimacy through consistency, and leading meaning-making in uncertain or crisis moments.

- [Influencing the Organisation](https://www.coursera.org/learn/influencing-the-organisation-leading-within-and-beyond-the-organisation): Covers managing stakeholders and organisational politics while mobilising people, and aligning your mission with the organisation's purpose.

## Udemy courses

- [Microcopy and UX Writing: The Complete Course](https://www.udemy.com/course/ux-writing/): Covers writing for common interface components including buttons, error messages, forms and empty states, and defining a consistent voice.

- [UX Writing: Microcopy, User Research, Accessibility and More](https://www.udemy.com/course/ux-writing-the-art-of-user-centered-copy/): Combines microcopy craft with the research and accessibility practice that should inform it.

- [Accessible UX Writing](https://www.udemy.com/course/accessible-ux-writing/): A focused course on writing inclusive and accessible copy, including alternative text, which is a common gap in content practice.

## MIT OpenCourseWare courses

- [15.317 Organizational Leadership and Change](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/15-317-organizational-leadership-and-change-summer-2009/): A Sloan course blending leadership theory and practice, in which participants reflect on prior leadership experience and apply the lessons to develop their capability further.

- [15.067 Competitive Decision-Making and Negotiation](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/15-067-competitive-decision-making-and-negotiation-spring-2011/): Develops negotiation skill through integrative bargaining cases between two and more than two parties over multiple issues.

## Blog posts

- [Content design: planning, writing and managing content](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/content-design/writing-for-gov-uk): The authoritative GOV.UK guidance, based on research into how people actually read online and use GOV.UK.

- [Content design on the GDS blog](https://gds.blog.gov.uk/category/content-design/): Posts from the team that codified the discipline, covering plain English, readability and writing for the web.

- [GOV.UK editorial style guide](https://gds.blog.gov.uk/2012/07/02/gov-uk-editorial-style-guide/): Explains the reasoning behind consistent rules for tone, language and presentation across government content.

- [Writing content for everyone](https://gds.blog.gov.uk/2016/02/23/writing-content-for-everyone/): Explains the reading age 9 standard and the finding that higher literacy readers also prefer plain English because it is faster to understand.

- [Designing content for people with limited English](https://hodigital.blog.gov.uk/2024/09/17/designing-content-for-people-with-limited-english/): Home Office Digital on designing for readers whose first language is not English, a group government content frequently fails.

- [Plain English: write less, say more](https://companieshouse.blog.gov.uk/2018/01/10/plain-english-write-less-say-more/): A departmental account of applying plain English to genuinely complex statutory content.

- [Writing and editing: plain language, ONS service manual](https://service-manual.ons.gov.uk/content/writing-for-users/plain-language): The Office for National Statistics guidance, particularly good on communicating statistics honestly.

- [Content accessibility: speak plainly](https://stormid.com/blog/content-accessibility-part-1-speak-plainly/): Connects plain language directly to accessibility requirements rather than treating them separately.

- [Unlocking Accessibility: Frontend developers discuss semantic HTML and accessible code](https://accessibility.blog.gov.uk/2025/04/25/unlocking-accessibility-frontend-developers-discuss-semantic-html-and-accessible-code/): Useful context on how content structure becomes accessible markup.

- [GOV.UK Design System accessibility strategy](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/accessibility/accessibility-strategy/): How the components your content sits inside are tested and maintained against WCAG.

## Research articles

- [Exploring the Effect of Plain Terminology on Processing and Comprehension of Administrative Texts](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/ijal.12650): A self-paced reading experiment testing whether plain terminology actually improves processing of administrative text, rather than assuming it does.

- [Jargon and Readability in Plain Language Summaries of Health Research: Cross-Sectional Observational Study](https://www.jmir.org/2025/1/e50862): Finds that only a small fraction of so-called plain language summaries actually reach plain English readability, and none matched average UK reading age.

- [Optimizing Readability and Format of Plain Language Summaries for Medical Research Articles](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8790687/): A survey study on what format and readability level readers actually prefer.

- [Scientific abstracts and plain language summaries in psychology: A comparison based on readability indices](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7117690/): Compares the two forms across readability measures, finding consistent improvement from plain language versions.

- [Readability in Policy Communication: Insights from a Bibliometric Analysis](https://journal.uitm.edu.my/ojs/index.php/JI/article/view/6146): Maps the research on readability in policy communication and where the evidence is strongest.

- [How compliance with web accessibility standards shapes the experiences of users with and without disabilities](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1071581922001756): Tests empirically whether standards compliance improves experience for real users.

- [Accessibility engineering in web evaluation process: a systematic literature review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10209-023-00967-2): Finds that most accessibility evaluation relies on automated tooling alone, with users rarely involved.

- [The Science of Visual Data Communication: What Works](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/15291006211051956): Synthesises the evidence on visual communication, relevant whenever content includes charts or diagrams.

- [Sample size in usability studies](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2133806.2133824): Useful for content designers planning content testing, since the familiar answer of five participants is often wrong.

- [To Intervene or Not to Intervene: An Investigation of Three Think-Aloud Protocols in Usability Testing](https://uxpajournal.org/intervene-think-aloud-protocols-usability-testing/): Compares think-aloud approaches, directly applicable to content testing sessions.

## Books on Amazon

- [Content Design](https://www.amazon.com/Content-Design-Sarah-Winters/dp/1916323316): By Sarah Winters, formerly Richards. Written by the person who codified the discipline while transforming over 400 government websites into GOV.UK. The foundational text.

- [Nicely Said: Writing for the Web with Style and Purpose](https://www.amazon.com/Nicely-Said-Writing-Purpose-Voices/dp/0321988191): By Nicole Fenton and Kate Lee. A practical guide to writing web content that meets reader needs while supporting organisational goals.

- [Web Accessibility Cookbook: Creating Inclusive Experiences](https://www.amazon.com/Web-Accessibility-Cookbook-Inclusive-Experiences-ebook/dp/B0D74TJF5B): By Manuel Matuzovic. Useful for content designers who need to understand how their content becomes accessible in practice.

- [Interviewing Users: How to Uncover Compelling Insights](https://www.amazon.com/Interviewing-Users-Uncover-Compelling-Insights/dp/1933820118): By Steve Portigal. Content decisions depend on understanding readers, and this is the best guide to the interview craft.

- [Observing the User Experience: A Practitioner's Guide to User Research](https://www.amazon.com/Observing-User-Experience-Practitioners-Research/dp/0123848695): Goodman, Kuniavsky and Moed. A methods reference for the research that should inform content decisions.

- [Storytelling with Data: A Data Visualization Guide for Business Professionals](https://www.amazon.com/Storytelling-Data-Visualization-Business-Professionals-ebook/dp/B016DHQSM2): Cole Nussbaumer Knaflic. Practical guidance for content that has to carry quantitative evidence.

- [User Story Mapping: Discover the Whole Story, Build the Right Product](https://www.amazon.com/User-Story-Mapping-Discover-Product-ebook/dp/B00NF07FHS): By Jeff Patton. A way of keeping the whole user journey in view while working on individual pieces.

- [Continuous Discovery Habits](https://www.amazon.com/Continuous-Discovery-Habits-Discover-Products-ebook/dp/B094PVB97X): By Teresa Torres. A system for continuous research alongside delivery, which content work benefits from as much as design.

- [Escaping the Build Trap: How Effective Product Management Creates Real Value](https://www.amazon.com/Escaping-Build-Trap-Effective-Management-ebook/dp/B07K3QBWG1): By Melissa Perri. On building an outcome-focused culture, which is the environment good content work needs.

- [Smashing UX Design: Foundations for Designing Online User Experiences](https://www.skillsoft.com/book/smashing-ux-design-foundations-for-designing-online-user-experiences-807d15f0-f050-11e6-8ce3-0242c0a80c07): A foundational guide across the whole design process, including the content layer.

## Professional development tips

- Set the organisation's content strategy and the standards that support it
- Judge the function by whether users can understand and act, not by content published
- Build content design as a discipline with a visible career path and real development
- Work with heads of design, research and product as genuine peers
- Make the case for content resourcing with evidence about failure demand and support costs
- Develop successors deliberately, since content leadership is hard to recruit
- Contribute to cross-government content communities, which set the standards you rely on
