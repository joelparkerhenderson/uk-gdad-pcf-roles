# Workplace job-specific verbal reasoning assessment

A workplace job-specific verbal reasoning assessment evaluates how well a candidate understands, analyses, and draws correct conclusions from written business information. It mirrors the exact reading and comprehension demands of the target role. [1, 2, 3, 4, 5]

## Key Characteristics

- Context-Specific Text: Passages use relevant industry terminology, reports, emails, or policy documents.
- Fact-Based Logic: Candidates must answer based strictly on the text provided, ignoring outside knowledge.
- Strict Options: Questions typically require a choice between True, False, or Cannot Say (insufficient information).
- Time-Pressured: Tests usually give less than one minute per question to simulate workplace deadlines. [6, 7, 8, 9, 10]

## How Different Roles Are Tested## 1. Legal Assistant (Compliance & Detail Focus)

- What it measures: Extreme precision, spotting contradictions, and identifying subtle contractual obligations.
- Test scenario: A dense paragraph from a commercial lease agreement regarding property maintenance and break clauses.
- Example question: "True, False, or Cannot Say: The tenant is financially liable for structural roof repairs if the lease is terminated within the first two years."

## 2. HR Administrator (Policy & Communication Focus)

- What it measures: Extracting rules from internal policies and applying them to staff queries.
- Test scenario: An excerpt from a company's updated maternity, paternity, and shared parental leave document.
- Example question: "True, False, or Cannot Say: Employees must submit their leave request form at least 28 days before their planned departure to qualify for full company top-up pay."

## 3. Public Relations Specialist (Nuance & Tone Focus)

- What it measures: Identifying the core argument, underlying sentiment, and hidden assumptions in text.
- Test scenario: A draft press release regarding a product recall or a transcript from a hostile media interview.
- Example question: "Which of the following statements best summarizes the company's official stance regarding the third-party supplier's liability?" [11, 12]

## Common Pitfalls for Candidates

- Making Assumptions: Bringing in personal knowledge instead of relying only on the provided text.
- Misinterpreting Nuance: Confusing a "likely outcome" stated in the text with an absolute "fact".
- Poor Time Management: Spending too long reading the whole text first instead of scanning for keywords.
