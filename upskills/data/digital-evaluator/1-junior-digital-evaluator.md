# Junior digital evaluator - upskill resources

This page focuses on learning and growth resources for the work role of junior digital evaluator.

- The resources include links to online courses, blogs, research articles, videos, and books.

- These help with job upskilling, work training, professional development, and career advancement.

- These are intended to be good starting points, not a comprehensive catalogue.

At this role level you support evaluation of digital services and interventions with guidance, learning evaluation methods, theory of change and how to present findings. The resources below emphasise evaluation fundamentals, government evaluation guidance and mixed methods.

We welcome constructive feedback to improve this page and these lists.

- GitHub issues and/or pull requests are welcome.

- The resources are found via AI searches then go through human-in-the-loop proofing.

## Coursera courses

- [Program Design and Evaluation](https://www.coursera.org/learn/program-design-and-evaluation-1): Covers how programme design, implementation and evaluation feedback combine to make a programme meet its stated objectives.

- [Measuring and Modeling Impact in Evaluations](https://www.coursera.org/learn/measuring-and-modeling-impact): From Johns Hopkins, covering the different measures of impact that can be used in evaluating a programme and the trade-offs between them.

- [Evaluating Large-Scale Health Programs](https://www.coursera.org/specializations/evaluating-large-scale-health-programs): A specialisation on planning and implementing effectiveness evaluations at scale; the methods generalise well beyond health.

## Udemy courses

- [Creating a Theory of Change and Logic Model](https://www.udemy.com/course/how-to-create-a-logic-model/): Explains who uses logic models, the definitions involved, and how to build one step by step as a management and communication tool for planning, monitoring and evaluating.

- [Essentials of Monitoring and Evaluation](https://www.udemy.com/course/essentials-of-monitoring-and-evaluation/): Covers the theory of change approach, articulating goals, assumptions and change pathways, and building a diagram of inputs, activities, outputs, outcomes and resources.

- [Impact Measurement in Monitoring and Evaluation](https://www.udemy.com/course/impact-evaluation-masterclass/): Covers practical and theoretical skills for rigorously evaluating the impact of projects and programmes across sectors.

- [Measuring Outcomes and Using KPIs](https://www.skillsoft.com/course/measuring-outcomes-and-using-kpis-baa51970-9bb8-11e8-a688-1150ad2aa658): Covers identifying performance problems through measurement systems and working with key performance indicators, in a short focused format.

- [Courses for Performance Management and Measurement](https://www.skillsoft.com/channel/performance-management-measurement-5ac2eb80-1ebf-11e7-9b8b-d7bb50ca1a50): A channel covering the practice of measuring and managing performance across an organisation.

## edX courses

- [Impact Evaluation Methods with Applications in Low- and Middle-Income Countries (GeorgetownX)](https://www.edx.org/course/impact-evaluation-methods-with-applications-in-low-and-middle-income-countries): Provides the experimental and statistical tools to measure programme effects, including designing and running randomised controlled trials and evaluating data with regression.

- [Evaluating Impact in Low- and Middle-Income Countries (WBGx)](https://www.edx.org/learn/economics/world-bank-group-impact-evaluation-methods-with-applications-in-low-and-middle-income-countries-2): The World Bank Group course covering experimental and statistical tools for measuring policy and programme effects, with a focus on causal attribution.

- [Data Analysis and Visualization Fundamentals Professional Certificate (IBM)](https://www.edx.org/certificates/professional-certificate/ibm-data-analysis-and-visualization-fundamentals): Covers the analytical and communication fundamentals that evaluation work depends on.

## MIT OpenCourseWare courses

- [15.075J Statistical Thinking and Data Analysis](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/15-075j-statistical-thinking-and-data-analysis-fall-2011/): Covers applied probability, sampling, estimation, hypothesis testing, regression and analysis of variance, which underpin any honest claim about whether performance changed.

- [14.310x Data Analysis for Social Scientists](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/14-310x-data-analysis-for-social-scientists-spring-2023/): Covers regression and econometrics, design of experiments, randomised controlled trials and visualisation, taught in R with policy applications.

- [15.060 Data, Models, and Decisions](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/15-060-data-models-and-decisions-fall-2014/): Covers how quantitative models support decisions, useful for keeping performance measurement tied to the decisions it should inform.

## Research articles

- [Methods in realist evaluation: A mapping review](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S014971892200163X): Maps how realist evaluation is actually conducted, which is useful given how loosely the label is often applied.

- [Realist evaluation in the policy design framework](https://www.elgaronline.com/edcollchap/book/9781800884892/book-part-9781800884892-10.xml): From the Handbook of Public Policy Evaluation, positioning realist evaluation within policy design.

- [Realist Evaluation, in Policy Evaluation: Methods and Approaches](https://scienceetbiencommun.pressbooks.pub/pubpolevaluation/chapter/realistic-evaluation/): An open textbook chapter explaining context-mechanism-outcome configurations and generative causation.

- [Assessing Public Policies: A Comprehensive Review of Evaluation Methods](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/385524691_Assessing_Public_Policies_A_Comprehensive_Review_of_Evaluation_Methods): Reviews formative, summative, results-based, impact, ex-ante and ex-post evaluation and when each applies.

- [An introduction to flexible methods for policy evaluation](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.00641): Covers modern approaches to estimating policy effects when randomisation is not available.

- [Applying causal inference to inform early-childhood policy from administrative data](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.06219): A worked example of causal inference on administrative data, the situation most government evaluators actually face.

- [Evaluating Program Sequences with Double Machine Learning: An Application to Labor Market Policies](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.11960): Applies modern causal machine learning to sequences of interventions rather than single treatments.

- [Seven pitfalls to avoid when running controlled experiments on the web](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/1557019.1557139): Kohavi and colleagues on the errors that make experiments produce confident but wrong answers.

- [Statistical Challenges in Online Controlled Experiments: A Review of A/B Testing Methodology](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/00031305.2023.2257237): A review of the recurring statistical problems in online experimentation.

- [Evaluating Ex Ante Counterfactual Predictions Using Ex Post Causal Inference](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1806.07016): Examines how well predicted counterfactuals hold up against later causal estimates, which bears on the credibility of ex ante appraisal.

## Books on Amazon

- [Qualitative Research and Evaluation Methods: Integrating Theory and Practice](https://www.amazon.com/Qualitative-Research-Evaluation-Methods-Integrating-ebook/dp/B00PDUHF5K): By Michael Quinn Patton. Draws on more than forty years of applied social science research and programme evaluation, with extended case studies. The standard reference for qualitative evaluation.

- [Facilitating Evaluation: Principles in Practice](https://us.amazon.com/Facilitating-Evaluation-Principles-Practice/dp/1506347614): By Michael Quinn Patton. Focuses on the facilitation skills evaluation requires, which are usually the binding constraint rather than method.

- [Evaluation in Practice](https://us.amazon.com/dp/B08CYWGNDN): Guides thinking about the design of mixed methods evaluations through worked examples and explanations.

- [Trustworthy Online Controlled Experiments: A Practical Guide to A/B Testing](https://www.amazon.com/Trustworthy-Online-Controlled-Experiments-Practical-ebook/dp/B0845Y3DJV): Kohavi, Tang and Xu. The standard reference for experimentation in digital services, drawing on tens of thousands of live experiments.

- [Storytelling with Data: A Data Visualization Guide for Business Professionals](https://www.amazon.com/Storytelling-Data-Visualization-Business-Professionals-ebook/dp/B016DHQSM2): Cole Nussbaumer Knaflic. Practical guidance on turning findings into an argument decision makers can follow.

- [Data Visualization: A Practical Introduction](https://www.amazon.com/Data-Visualization-Introduction-Kieran-Healy/dp/0691181624): Kieran Healy. Grounds visualisation choices in perception research, worked through in R.

- [Lean Analytics: Use Data to Build a Better Startup Faster](https://www.amazon.com/Lean-Analytics-Better-Startup-Faster-ebook/dp/B00AG66LTM): Croll and Yoskovitz. The discipline of choosing the one metric that matters right now transfers well to evaluating iterative service work.

- [Python Data Science Handbook: Essential Tools for Working with Data](https://www.amazon.com/Python-Data-Science-Handbook-Essential/dp/1098121228): Jake VanderPlas. Covers the analytical toolchain for evaluators working with substantial datasets.

- [Data Science from Scratch: First Principles with Python](https://www.amazon.com/Data-Science-Scratch-Principles-Python/dp/149190142X): Joel Grus. Builds genuine understanding of the methods rather than tool familiarity.

- [The Visual Display of Quantitative Information](https://www.amazon.com/Visual-Display-Quantitative-Information/dp/1930824130): Edward Tufte. The foundational text on presenting quantitative evidence honestly and clearly.

## Professional development tips

- Read the Magenta Book properly, since it is the standard your evaluations will be judged against
- Learn to write a theory of change before choosing any method, because method follows question
- Practise distinguishing outputs, outcomes and impacts precisely, since they are constantly conflated
- Build basic statistical literacy so you can tell a real effect from noise
- Sit with service teams to understand what actually happens, not what the documentation says happens
- Learn both qualitative and quantitative methods, since good evaluation almost always needs both
- Ask for feedback on your reports from the people expected to act on them
