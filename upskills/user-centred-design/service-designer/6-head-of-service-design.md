# Head of service design - upskill resources

This page focuses on learning and growth resources for the work role of head of service design.

- The resources include links to online courses, blogs, research articles, videos, and books.

- These help with job upskilling, work training, professional development, and career advancement.

- These are intended to be good starting points, not a comprehensive catalogue.

At this role level you are accountable for service design across the organisation, its approach, its capability and its influence on whole services. The resources below emphasise organisational direction, discipline building and executive influence.

We welcome constructive feedback to improve this page and these lists.

- GitHub issues and/or pull requests are welcome.

- The resources are found via AI searches then go through human-in-the-loop proofing.

- For more contact Joel Henderson <joel@joelparkerhenderson.com>

## Coursera courses

- [Strategic Leadership: Impact, Change, and Decision-Making](https://www.coursera.org/specializations/strategic-leadership-impact-change-decision-making): A Dartmouth Tuck specialisation with Sydney Finkelstein covering the mistakes smart executives make, the neuroscience behind effective decision making, and how to manage and support the people around you.

- [Strategic Leadership: Influence and Thought Leadership](https://www.coursera.org/learn/strategic-leadership-influence-thought-leadership): Covers high-level influence techniques used by senior leaders, including mapping influence networks, strong framing, building legitimacy through consistency, and leading meaning-making in uncertain or crisis moments.

- [Influencing the Organisation](https://www.coursera.org/learn/influencing-the-organisation-leading-within-and-beyond-the-organisation): Covers managing stakeholders and organisational politics while mobilising people, and aligning your mission with the organisation's purpose.

## Udemy courses

- [User Experience (UX): The Ultimate Guide to Usability](https://www.udemy.com/course/ultimate-guide-to-ux/): Hands-on practice across the key areas of user experience work, from interviewing users through prototyping to usability testing.

- [UX Design Process from User Research to Usability Testing](https://www.udemy.com/course/ux-design-process-for-beginners-from-user-research-to-usability/): Covers the whole process from user interviews and personas through user stories and information architecture to usability testing.

- [Mastering UX Research: From Fundamentals to Job Skills](https://www.udemy.com/course/ux-research-bootcamp-from-zero-experience-to-job-ready/): Covers strategic research planning, interviews, analysis and deliverables, with both qualitative and quantitative methods including usability testing, surveys, card sorting and tree testing.

## Blog posts

- [Service mapping: a step by step guide](https://services.blog.gov.uk/2020/09/01/service-mapping-a-step-by-step-guide/): UK government guidance on mapping a user's whole problem, and how understanding the wider journey helps teams move between the big picture and the detail.

- [Service landscape maps: a tool for mapping public services](https://services.blog.gov.uk/2022/03/23/service-landscape-maps-a-tool-for-mapping-public-services): Introduces a map type for building collective understanding of what government offers across a whole service area.

- [How to make a user journey map](https://designnotes.blog.gov.uk/2016/04/21/how-to-make-a-user-journey-map/): From the head of design at the Government Digital Service, on producing journey maps that are actually used rather than admired.

- [How we approached service mapping](https://insidegovuk.blog.gov.uk/2018/02/07/how-we-approached-service-mapping/): An account of service mapping behind GOV.UK step-by-step navigation, with the practical decisions involved.

- [Service mapping and different types of maps](https://medium.com/leading-service-design/service-mapping-and-different-types-of-maps-604a1a22e22c): Ben Holliday on the different map types and what each is actually for, which prevents a lot of wasted effort.

- [Service Blueprint vs Journey Map: What's the difference?](https://outwitly.com/blog/customer-journey-maps-vs-service-blueprints/): Distinguishes two artefacts that are constantly conflated, and explains when each is appropriate.

- [Citizen journey mapping in government](https://www.smaply.com/blog/journey-mapping-government): Covers using journey mapping to build internal buy-in and to map front and backstage processes in a public sector setting.

- [Use journey maps to understand and improve people's interactions with public services](https://www.govloop.com/community/blog/use-journey-maps-to-understand-and-improve-peoples-interactions-with-public-services/): A practical treatment of recording experiences and pain points to generate improvement.

- [Metrics and measurement on the Services in government blog](https://services.blog.gov.uk/category/metrics-and-measurement/): How service teams measure whether a service actually works for users.

- [Data you must publish, GOV.UK Service Manual](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/measuring-success/data-you-must-publish): The mandatory service measures, which give service designers a common language with delivery and policy colleagues.

## Research articles

- [Design for experience: a public service design approach in the age of digitalization](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/14719037.2021.1899272): Examines co-production and user experience concepts together in public management, and where the two literatures diverge.

- [A Scoping Review of Empirical Evidence on Digital Public Services Co-Creation](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3387/11/4/130): Reviews 25 empirical studies on co-creating digital public services and what conditions make it work.

- [A Comparative Analysis of Co-Production in Public Services](https://doi.org/10.3390/su13126730): Examines 32 studies and classifies forms of co-production, finding that citizen-centric strategies perform better.

- [Understanding Service Design and Design Thinking Differences Between Research and Practice: An Empirical Study](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/338838119_Understanding_Service_Design_and_Design_Thinking_Differences_Between_Research_and_Practice_An_Empirical_Study): Documents the gap between how service design is described in research and how it is practised.

- [Co-designing Healthcare Services with Patients](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-53705-0_16): A worked account of co-design in a high-stakes public service setting.

- [How compliance with web accessibility standards shapes the experiences of users with and without disabilities](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1071581922001756): Tests empirically whether standards compliance improves experience for real users.

- [Understanding how agile teams reach effectiveness: A systematic literature review](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1053482224000469): Reviews the evidence on what makes multidisciplinary teams effective, which shapes how service design is best embedded.

- [Achieving benefits with enterprise architecture](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0963868716302360): Finds that benefits come from advisory services rather than artefacts, a lesson that applies directly to service maps.

- [Sample size in usability studies](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2133806.2133824): Useful when planning research to support service design decisions, since the familiar answer of five participants is often wrong.

- [Challenges of Government Enterprise Architecture Work: Stakeholders' Views](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-540-85204-9_31): An empirical government study on why cross-organisational design work stalls, which service designers encounter constantly.

## Professional development tips

- Set the organisation's approach to service design and where it should be applied
- Judge the function by whole services that work for users, not by maps and workshops delivered
- Build service design as a discipline with a visible career path and real development
- Work with policy, operations and digital leadership as genuine peers, since none can fix services alone
- Make the case for cross-organisational service ownership, which is the underlying structural problem
- Develop successors deliberately, since service design leadership is hard to recruit
- Contribute to cross-government service design communities, since most journeys cross departments
