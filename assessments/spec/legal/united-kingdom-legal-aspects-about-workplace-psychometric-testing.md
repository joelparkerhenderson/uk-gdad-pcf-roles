# United Kingdom legal aspects about workplace psychometric assessment

In the [United Kingdom](https://www.google.com/search?kgmid=/m/07ssc), workplace IQ testing is legal but strictly regulated under equality and data protection laws. There is no statutory ban on psychometric or cognitive testing. However, if an employer uses an IQ test that disproportionately disadvantages individuals with specific protected characteristics, they risk face costly indirect discrimination claims at an [Employment Tribunal](https://www.gov.uk/courts-tribunals/employment-tribunal). [1, 2, 3]
------------------------------

## Core Statutory Frameworks

- Equality Act 2010: This is the primary legislation governing workplace testing. Employers must ensure that cognitive tests do not create an unfair barrier based on protected characteristics like race, sex, age, or disability. [4, 5, 6]
- UK GDPR and Data Protection Act 2018: IQ scores constitute personal data. Under Information Commissioner's Office (ICO) guidelines, employers must establish a lawful basis for processing this data, ensure test transparency, and securely store or delete the results. [7]
- The "Reasonable Adjustments" Duty: Under Section 20 of the Equality Act 2010, employers have a proactive legal duty to modify the testing process for disabled or neurodivergent candidates (e.g., individuals with dyslexia, autism, or ADHD). [8, 9]

---

## Legal Risk: Indirect Discrimination & Justification

Unlike US law, which relies on a strict mathematical "80% rule," UK law uses a broader concept of disproportionate disadvantage. If a job applicant can prove an IQ test puts a protected group at a disadvantage, the employer faces a strict legal hurdle:

[Test Disadvantages Protected Group] ➔ [Employer Must Prove Objective Justification] ➔ [Must Be a Proportionate Means of Achieving a Legitimate Aim]

To legally defend the test, the employer must prove Objective Justification. This requires demonstrating that the test is a "proportionate means of achieving a legitimate aim." [10, 11, 12, 13]

- Legitimate Aim: The employer must prove the test measures an authentic, business-critical requirement (e.g., high-level logical reasoning for a complex quantitative data role).
- Proportionate: The employer must show that the test is appropriate and necessary. If a claimant proves the employer could have assessed capability through a less discriminatory method—such as a tailored portfolio review or practical trade test—the employer will lose the tribunal case. [14, 15]

---

## Neurodiversity and the Duty to Adjust

Generic IQ tests carry the highest legal risk in the UK regarding disability discrimination. If an employer administers a standard, timed abstract reasoning test without offering alterations, they face immediate liability. Legal compliance requires providing tailored accommodations: [16]

- Extra Time: Providing an additional 25% (or more) time for candidates with reading or processing difficulties.
- Alternative Formats: Offering the assessment via text-to-speech software, adjusted color contrasts, or paper-based formats.
- Exemptions: Bypassing the generic IQ test entirely in favour of a structured work sample if the psychometric format itself unfairly penalises a neurodivergent applicant.

---

## Best Practices: The British Psychological Society (BPS) Guidelines

To minimise legal exposure, UK employers rarely use raw "IQ tests." Instead, they align assessments with guidelines from the British Psychological Society (BPS) Psychological Testing Centre:

| Test Category               | Target Metrics                                             | Legal Risk | Risk Mitigation Strategy                                                             |
| --------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ---------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| General IQ / Cognitive      | Abstract logic, fluid intelligence                         | High       | Ensure a formal job analysis links fluid intelligence to daily performance.          |
| Job-Specific Aptitude       | Numerical data interpretation, verbal situational analysis | Medium     | Tie test scenarios explicitly to actual workplace documentation or case studies.     |
| Situational Judgement (SJT) | Workplace decision-making, behavioral tendencies           | Low        | Map questions directly to the employer's public, internal core competency framework. |
