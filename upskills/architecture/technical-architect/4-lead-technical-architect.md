# Lead technical architect - upskill resources

This page focuses on learning and growth resources for the work role of lead technical architect.

- The resources include links to online courses, blogs, research articles, videos, and books.

- These help with job upskilling, work training, professional development, and career advancement.

- These are intended to be good starting points, not a comprehensive catalogue.

At this role level you work with multiple projects or teams on problems that require broad architectural thinking, setting direction and supporting other architects. The resources below emphasise leadership, guardrails and patterns, and managing architectural risk across a portfolio.

We welcome constructive feedback to improve this page and these lists.

- GitHub issues and/or pull requests are welcome.

- The resources are found via AI searches then go through human-in-the-loop proofing.

## Coursera courses

- [Strategic Leadership: Impact, Change, and Decision-Making](https://www.coursera.org/specializations/strategic-leadership-impact-change-decision-making): A Dartmouth Tuck specialisation with Sydney Finkelstein covering the mistakes smart executives make, the neuroscience behind effective decision making, and how to manage and support the people around you.

- [Strategic Leadership: Influence and Thought Leadership](https://www.coursera.org/learn/strategic-leadership-influence-thought-leadership): Covers high-level influence techniques used by senior leaders, including mapping influence networks, strong framing, building legitimacy through consistency, and leading meaning-making in uncertain or crisis moments.

- [Influencing the Organisation](https://www.coursera.org/learn/influencing-the-organisation-leading-within-and-beyond-the-organisation): Covers managing stakeholders and organisational politics while mobilising people, and aligning your mission with the organisation's purpose.

## Udemy courses

- [Software Architecture and Design of Modern Large Scale Systems](https://www.udemy.com/course/software-architecture-design-of-modern-large-scale-systems/): A practical course on architecting real systems that scale to millions of daily users and process large volumes of data.

- [Mastering Software Architecture Patterns and System Design](https://www.udemy.com/course/mastering-software-architecture-patterns-for-system-design/): Explores hexagonal, clean and onion architectures alongside distributed models including microservices, service-oriented architecture and event-driven systems, plus API gateways, CQRS, event sourcing, containers and serverless.

- [Design Microservices Architecture with Patterns and Principles](https://www.udemy.com/course/design-microservices-architecture-with-patterns-principles/): Traces the evolution from monolithic to event-driven microservices, designing for high availability, high scalability, low latency and resilience to network failure.

## Research articles

- [Identifying architectural technical debt, principal, and interest in microservices: A multiple-case study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0164121221000650): Based on 25 interviews with practitioners in seven large companies, this study identifies 16 architectural technical debt issues, their negative impact, and common repayment solutions with costs.

- [Microservices, Continuous Architecture, and Technical Debt Interest: An Empirical Study](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/326059322_Microservices_Continuous_Architecture_and_Technical_Debt_Interest_An_Empirical_Study): A case study of an organisation migrating from a monolith to microservices, finding that total technical debt grew much faster in the microservices system, probably because refactoring was postponed.

- [The technical debt gamble: A case study on technical debt in a large-scale industrial microservice architecture](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S016412122500216X): A mixed-method study combining static analysis with developer focus groups and interviews with the lead architect.

- [Exploring the evolution of technical debt in monolithic and hybrid microservice architecture: An industrial case study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0164121226000658): Provides empirical observations relevant to organisations considering migration from monolithic to microservice architecture.

- [Temporal Evolution of Architectural Complexity and Technical Debt in Microservices: An Exploratory Case Study](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-032-12089-2_18): Examines the relationship between architectural complexity indicators, including declared dependencies, API endpoints, inter-service communication and size, and technical debt over time.

- [TOGAF-based Enterprise Architecture Practice: An Exploratory Case Study](https://aisel.aisnet.org/cais/vol43/iss1/20/): Shows how organisations adapt architecture methodologies in practice, and how far real practice diverges from the published method.

- [Achieving benefits with enterprise architecture](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0963868716302360): Argues that architecture benefits are realised indirectly through advisory services within change projects rather than through the architecture artefacts themselves.

- [A theory building study of enterprise architecture practices and benefits](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10796-014-9542-1): A 293-respondent survey showing that architecture works through intermediate results, most notably compliance and architectural insight.

- [Stakeholder Perception of Enterprise Architecture](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/220757098_Stakeholder_Perception_of_Enterprise_Architecture): Finds that stakeholders often perceive architecture as abstract and difficult to comprehend, and links governance and communication practice to perceived value.

- [Enterprise Architecture and IT Governance to Support the BizDevOps Approach: a Systematic Mapping Study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10796-024-10473-2): Maps the literature on architecture and governance in continuous delivery settings, where solution architecture must keep pace with deployment.

## Professional development tips

- Work across projects by setting guardrails and patterns rather than by attending every design discussion
- Make architectural decisions promptly and record them, since delayed decisions cost delivery teams the most
- Develop your architects by giving them genuine accountability for whole areas
- Manage architectural risk as a portfolio across the teams you support, not project by project
- Build the relationships with enterprise architecture and with delivery leadership that keep teams unblocked
- Translate technical trade-offs into time, cost and risk for the people who fund the work
- Invest in the cross-government community, since shared patterns reduce cost for everyone
