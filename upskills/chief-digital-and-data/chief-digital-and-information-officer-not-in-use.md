# Chief digital and information officer - upskill resources

This page focuses on learning and growth resources for the work role of chief digital and information officer.

- The resources include links to online courses, blogs, research articles, videos, and books.

- These help with job upskilling, work training, professional development, and career advancement.

- These are intended to be good starting points, not a comprehensive catalogue.

This role shapes and enables delivery of the organisation's strategy through digital, data and technology, leading innovative strategies, securing investment, ensuring security and resilience, and acting as trusted advisor to the board. The role level in the framework is recorded as NOT IN USE. The resources below emphasise executive digital leadership, strategy and investment.

We welcome constructive feedback to improve this page and these lists.

- GitHub issues and/or pull requests are welcome.

- The resources are found via AI searches then go through human-in-the-loop proofing.

## Research articles

- [The role of CIO in digital transformation: an exploratory study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10257-023-00651-1): A multiple case study of six organisations engaged in digital transformation, positioning the CIO as an actor focused on digital strategy and setting out the multidimensional task demands the role faces.

- [Digital leadership in the public sector: a scoping review and outlook](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/12294659.2024.2323847): Reviews what is known about digital leadership specifically in public sector settings, and where the evidence is thin.

- [Digital Leadership Competency to Enhance Digital Transformation](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/379764371_Digital_Leadership_Competency_to_Enhance_Digital_Transformation): An empirical study of 433 respondents conceptualising and validating digital leadership competency and measuring its effect on organisational transformation.

- [Chief Digital Officers as Protagonists in Digital Transformation](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-29374-1_59): Examines what chief digital officers actually do and how the role interacts with existing technology leadership.

- [Exploring the evolution of leadership in government: a bibliometric study from e-government era into the digital age](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/23311886.2024.2414877): Traces how the literature on government digital leadership has developed, and identifies governance, strategic IT leadership and cross-sector collaboration as recurring themes.

- [Digital Leadership and Transformation in Contemporary Times](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/383178594_Digital_Leadership_and_Transformation_in_Contemporary_Times): A broad treatment of digital leadership practice and the conditions under which it succeeds.

- [Achieving benefits with enterprise architecture](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0963868716302360): Finds that architecture benefits are largely intangible and realised indirectly within change projects, which matters when justifying investment in technology functions.

- [A theory building study of enterprise architecture practices and benefits](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10796-014-9542-1): A 293-respondent study showing that architecture works through intermediate results rather than delivering benefits directly.

- [Challenges of Government Enterprise Architecture Work: Stakeholders' Views](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-540-85204-9_31): An empirical government study concluding that governance must be adequately adjusted and enforced as a tool for business operations development.

- [Enterprise Architecture and IT Governance to Support the BizDevOps Approach: a Systematic Mapping Study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10796-024-10473-2): Maps the literature on architecture and governance in continuous delivery environments, noting a shortage of empirical validation in the field.

## Professional development tips

- Spend deliberate time with the board in their language, since digital advice is only useful if it is heard
- Judge the function by outcomes for users and by resilience, not by the volume of technology delivered
- Build a small number of durable partnerships across the organisation rather than many shallow ones
- Insist that architecture decisions are tested for scalability, reusability and interoperability across government
- Keep a live investment case that survives contact with a spending review
- Grow the capability deliberately, since the constraint is almost always people rather than technology
- Anticipate the changing landscape rather than reacting to it, and be explicit about the bets you are placing
