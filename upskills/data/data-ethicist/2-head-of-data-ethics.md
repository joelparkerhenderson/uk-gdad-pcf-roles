# Head of data ethics - upskill resources

This page focuses on learning and growth resources for the work role of head of data ethics.

- The resources include links to online courses, blogs, research articles, videos, and books.

- These help with job upskilling, work training, professional development, and career advancement.

- These are intended to be good starting points, not a comprehensive catalogue.

At this role level you are accountable for the organisation's data ethics function, its framework, its independence and its influence on how data and algorithmic systems are built and used. The resources below emphasise ethical governance, organisational leadership and capability building.

We welcome constructive feedback to improve this page and these lists.

- GitHub issues and/or pull requests are welcome.

- The resources are found via AI searches then go through human-in-the-loop proofing.

## Coursera courses

- [Strategic Leadership: Impact, Change, and Decision-Making](https://www.coursera.org/specializations/strategic-leadership-impact-change-decision-making): A Dartmouth Tuck specialisation with Sydney Finkelstein covering the mistakes smart executives make, the neuroscience behind effective decision making, and how to manage and support the people around you.

- [Strategic Leadership: Influence and Thought Leadership](https://www.coursera.org/learn/strategic-leadership-influence-thought-leadership): Covers high-level influence techniques used by senior leaders, including mapping influence networks, strong framing, building legitimacy through consistency, and leading meaning-making in uncertain or crisis moments.

- [Influencing the Organisation](https://www.coursera.org/learn/influencing-the-organisation-leading-within-and-beyond-the-organisation): Covers managing stakeholders and organisational politics while mobilising people, and aligning your mission with the organisation's purpose.

## Udemy courses

- [AI Ethics and Responsible AI: Fairness, Bias, and Governance](https://www.udemy.com/course/ethicsai/): Covers the core concepts of fairness, bias, transparency and accountability and how they are operationalised in governance.

- [Certified AI Ethics and Governance Professional](https://www.udemy.com/course/certified-ai-ethics-governance-professional-a/): Covers responsible AI development in depth, including bias, fairness, transparency, accountability and privacy, with practical application.

- [Responsible AI Masterclass: Ethics, Fairness and Governance](https://www.udemy.com/course/responsible-ai-masterclass-ethics-bias-governance/): Covers the foundational pillars of responsible AI, recognising types of bias, and designing governance frameworks for organisational accountability and legal compliance.

## MIT OpenCourseWare courses

- [15.317 Organizational Leadership and Change](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/15-317-organizational-leadership-and-change-summer-2009/): A Sloan course blending leadership theory and practice, in which participants reflect on prior leadership experience and apply the lessons to develop their capability further.

- [15.067 Competitive Decision-Making and Negotiation](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/15-067-competitive-decision-making-and-negotiation-spring-2011/): Develops negotiation skill through integrative bargaining cases between two and more than two parties over multiple issues.

## Blog posts

- [Data Ethics on the Data in Government blog](https://dataingovernment.blog.gov.uk/category/data-ethics/): The UK government blog category on data ethics, covering how ethical questions are handled in practice across departments.

- [Data governance on the Government Data Architecture blog](https://dataarchitecture.blog.gov.uk/category/data-architecture/data-governance/): Covers the governance mechanisms through which ethical commitments become enforceable.

- [Data Standards on the Data in Government blog](https://dataingovernment.blog.gov.uk/category/data-standards/): Includes coverage of the Algorithmic Transparency Recording Standard, which became mandatory for central government.

- [Ethics in Data Analysis and Modeling](https://openstax.org/books/principles-data-science/pages/8-2-ethics-in-data-analysis-and-modeling): An open textbook chapter on ethical questions specific to analysis and modelling rather than to data collection.

- [Machine Learning Ethics: Understanding Bias and Fairness](https://www.vationventures.com/research-article/machine-learning-ethics-understanding-bias-and-fairness): A practitioner overview of how bias enters models and what mitigation actually involves.

- [Reproducible data science](https://domino.ai/blog/reproducible-data-science): Explains why full documentation of a model's development and use is necessary for transparency, justification and validation, which is a precondition for ethical scrutiny.

- [Data Governance and Metadata Management: A Vital Duo for Data-Driven Success](https://atlan.com/data-governance-and-metadata-management/): Explains why ethical commitments without metadata infrastructure are hard to evidence.

- [Data Stewardship: The Keystone to Enable Data Governance](https://guidehouse.com/insights/advanced-solutions/2023/data-stewardship-to-enable-data-governance): Argues that stewardship is the operational layer without which policy, including ethical policy, has no effect.

- [Zero Trust is not just an architecture](https://www.nexor.com/blog/zero-trust-is-not-just-an-architecture): Useful framing on designing protective properties in from the outset rather than adding them later, which applies equally to ethical safeguards.

- [Fundamentals of Metadata Management](https://www.dataversity.net/fundamentals-metadata-management/): Covers lineage and provenance, without which questions about how a decision was reached cannot be answered.

## Research articles

- [Fairness Perceptions of Algorithmic Decision-Making: A Systematic Review of the Empirical Literature](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/20539517221115189): Starke, Baleis, Keller and Marcinkowski review the empirical evidence on how people actually perceive algorithmic fairness, which often diverges from formal fairness metrics.

- [Fairness Perceptions of Algorithmic Decision-Making (preprint)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.12016): The open-access version of the above systematic review.

- [Perceived Algorithmic Fairness using Organizational Justice Theory: An Empirical Case Study on Algorithmic Hiring](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3600211.3604677): From the AAAI/ACM Conference on AI, Ethics and Society, applying organisational justice theory to a high-stakes automated decision context.

- [Algorithmic Fairness Datasets: the Story so Far](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.01711): Documents the datasets on which fairness research rests and their significant limitations, which matters when citing fairness results.

- [Statistical and Structural Approaches to Algorithmic Fairness](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.26200): Contrasts statistical fairness criteria with structural accounts of why disparities arise, which leads to different interventions.

- [Reducing the incidence of biased algorithmic decisions through feature importance transparency: an empirical study](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/0960085X.2024.2395531): Tests whether transparency actually reduces users' adoption of discriminatory recommendations, rather than assuming it does.

- [Algorithmic fairness in computational medicine](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9463525/): Reviews types of algorithmic bias, fairness quantification metrics and mitigation methods in a high-stakes public service context.

- [Ethical and Bias Considerations in Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0893395224002667): A practical review of the ethical and bias questions that arise across the machine learning lifecycle.

- [Locating ethics in data science: responsibility and accountability in global and distributed knowledge production systems](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5124067/): Examines where responsibility actually sits when analysis is produced by distributed teams, which is the central problem in assigning accountability.

- [Leakage and the reproducibility crisis in machine-learning-based science](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10499856/): Documents how methodological failure produces overconfident results, which is an ethical problem as much as a technical one.

## Professional development tips

- Set an ethical framework that produces decisions rather than principles nobody can apply
- Build the organisational conditions for ethical practice, including time, access and the ability to say stop
- Protect the independence of ethical advice, since its value depends on it being uncomfortable sometimes
- Develop ethical capability across delivery teams rather than concentrating it in a specialist function
- Represent data ethics at senior level, including when the finding is unwelcome
- Work with peers across government to share approaches and precedents
- Track what happened to systems you advised on, since ethical assessment is only meaningful with follow-through
